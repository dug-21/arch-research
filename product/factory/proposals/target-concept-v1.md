# Ultimate Workflow
Agentic Enterprise:  We build the organization, communication pathways, structured autonomy, execution pathways, and governance, all with a durable memory substrate.  If you think of the analogy, it is how to create a continuously improving, thinking organization, for execution.  Think of it like building an agentic USS enterprise (to go along with the Unimatrix theme)

Our main product is building software products, but the focus is to continously be on a leading edge, with the fortitude to try things that may not have been tried before... take proven concepts from adjacent places where it may make sense.  For a full organization to function, however, it operates in a distributed fashion, independent actions, but with a goal of ensuring the key communication channels still have centralized intelligence.  It is in this type of organization, change of direction can be immediate if needed.  The scope of our future is not solely on building software... it could be anything.  So, our core foundation is about building things, thinking, learning, and the governance platform that will keep it from trying to conquer our allies.

## Core Principle
I admire a guy by the name of Dean Kamen.  He is the inventor of the Segway, as well at home dialysis machine, and many many others.  He once told a story about how, when he was working on a new medical device, where he needed a strong tube that could be inserted into a human body (it needed to be flexible & not collapse), he didn't solve that because he was the expert.  What he did was to find an expert in human physiology, and pare that with an expert from the automobile industry (knows metal properties, tubing structure, etc).  So by combining skillsets in unique ways that don't seem related, he was able to lead teams to some amazing inventions.

I'd like my organization to be based on exactly this type of principle.  I like to try new things... not afraid to fail, and apply skills and methods sometimes created long ago to see if they are applicable to today.

## Structure/Methodology
I want to run a fairly centralized program.  (In much of its early phases, Apple's leadership/decisions was driven by 7 people around a table).  But establish the mechanisms by which the workload gets distributed.   There are fundamental mechanisms to put in place, comms channels... what data needs to be centralized... how to centralize it, etc.  we'll need to decide how to manage R&D consistently... how to continue to build a repository of knowledge that can be leveraged everywhere, how projects communicate with one another.  Example:  our research and development garage has identified bugs and opportunities for Unimatrix to evolve... I can see this happening more overtime... this inter team comms mechanism need to get established.

## Governance/Checks and balances
The system needs to account for and protect against Halucinations, agents going astray, and other well established risks of agentic behavior... false claims of success... hardcoding solutions, etc.  There needs to be a way of managing this type of behavior..  Provie your work as a philosophy.

## Security
Long term... this is a high security operation.  Short term... we need to architect in the right security seams, and build them over time.

## Determinism vs Non-deterministic
The philosophy, is leverage intelligent deterministic capability WHEREVER possible... and limit the use of LLM's or non-deterministic results.  This still leaves a huge space for non-deterministic behaviors... but the governance/gates/control plane needs to be out of reach from the Agentic activity.  Agents cannot be able to change the rules in real time to solve their problems.

## Build vs Buy
We can assemble using components (not ruv's).  I think of it like MVP.  My long term goal is run this almost all autnonomously outside of publicly served LLM's.  This is not possible today, but using tools that build that flexibility into our platofmr now... takes us further, faster.  If I want to come back and replace that functionality with custom component for some reason, that becomes easier once we have a functional system.  I don't mind building things.  I like buiding things that are highly efficient (rust where it makes sense), and somewhere near the edge... but, to get started faster I'll accept some inefficiencies.
