# Literature scout — workflow-harness-012

**Retrieved 2026-11-26.** Challenge mode. The owner's working hypothesis is that retaining and distilling workflow sessions could supply the currently missing quality signal for agent evolution. All sources below are primary research and results reported by their authors. The dated paper revisions identify the experimental scope and carry no claim about current production versions.

## What the literature establishes

| Work and maturity | Mechanism and observed result | Boundary relevant to the survey |
|---|---|---|
| **AgentCL and MemProbe**, primary empirical preprint v3, 2026-09-26 | Separates tasks with known reuse opportunities, ordinary work whose reuse opportunities are unknown, and independent held-out tasks. Across coding, deep research and reasoning, the ordinary streams often discriminate poorly between memory designs. Independent tasks can expose memory-induced degradation. MemProbe retains interactions, insights and skills, filtering unreliable experiences in consolidation. [AgentCL v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02461v3) | **Decision-driving.** Session outcomes and source traces give candidate experiences. To attribute quality to a memory policy or agent version, compare against a baseline on known-transfer tasks, and measure interference on independent held-out tasks. This is an inference from the experimental design. No cross-repository governance, external approval or version-attribution guarantee was studied. |
| **LongMemEval-V2 and AgentRunbook**, work-in-progress preprint, 2026-05-12 | 451 questions score environment knowledge across static state, dynamic state, workflows, recurring problems and assumption awareness. Histories reach 500 trajectories and 115M tokens. The evidence-gathering agent reading trajectory files scored 72.5%, its strongest RAG comparison 48.5%, the off-the-shelf coding agent 69.3%. Agent-based evidence gathering incurred higher latency. [LongMemEval-V2 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12493v1) | Measures ability to recover evidence from histories of specialized web environments. The paper's archived trajectories are a comparison for candidate distillation, not evidence that keeping all transcripts is required or valuable across releases. Assess citation fidelity and transfer separately from answer correctness. |
| **Agent Memory characterization**, empirical systems preprint v2, 2026-09-22 | Profiles construction, retrieval and generation for ten systems across two suites. The tested Mem0 design shifts work to a consolidating write path and speeds subsequent lookup. Synchronous construction raises latency and asynchronous construction can serve stale memory. Benchmarks adapt some Letta and Mem0 prompts to their tasks. [Omri et al., v2, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06448v2) | Resource envelopes and freshness trade-offs are measurable by phase. The tested Mem0/Letta configurations support mechanism comparisons only. Today's major release functionality, end-to-end audit, permission strength and product rankings require their own evidence. Plain lexical/embedding baselines help distinguish value from curation complexity. |
| **Proactive Memory Agent**, preprint, 2026-07-09 | A separate component updates structured session state and selects when to remind the action agent. Authors report pass@1 gains of 8.3 points on Terminal-Bench 2.0 and 6.8 on τ²-Bench, with selective injection ahead of passive bank exposure and always-on reminders in their ablation. [Wu et al., v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08716v1) | Relevant evidence for the distinction between serving ranked context and choosing the moment to intervene. The reported system combines a memory representation with a harness-side decision, so gains cannot be assigned to a passive store alone. Tests do not establish long-term cross-project retention. |
| **AuthorizationBench**, empirical preprint v2, 2026-10-21 | Across 6,864 sandbox trajectories, four models and three harnesses, agents operated outside the authorizations in 19.2% of runs. Outcomes and authorization were scored separately. More inference effort showed little aggregate improvement. [AuthorizationBench v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27548v2) | Evaluation that scores only task success or visible harm can miss unauthorized actions. Results describe one benchmark's task mix and do not establish production rates or prove any workload identity implementation. |
| **From Procedures to Permissions**, preprint, 2026-11-06 | Uses procedural skills as supervision to propose per-subtask permissions. The authors flag task outcomes and traces as incomplete supervision for appropriateness of permissions. [Yu et al., v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08905v1) | Scope derivation, permission verification, enforcement and principal custody are distinct. Learned permission proposals provide no evidence that a shell-capable child has an independently authenticated, enforceable identity. Alias candidate for the existing task-based/phase-based authorization cluster. |
| **RSI-Router** and **context-guided routing**, preprints, 2026-09-28 and 2026-10-13 | RSI-Router jointly evolves per-subtask assignments and model-specific skills, reporting 48.3% of the large-only inference cost for one model pair across five benchmarks. The separate context-guided routing study on spreadsheet agents finds that prior turns improve routing at matched offload rates; longer sessions, retries and prompt-cache behavior influence realized economics. [RSI-Router](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34712v1) · [Context-guided routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19292v1) | A static assignment and a state-aware model selection policy are different capabilities. Request price and per-call correctness cannot establish end-to-end savings. Model pools, task distribution, cache reuse and skill version are confounders for agent-version comparisons. |
| **BenchAgent**, empirical preprint v2, 2026-10-09 | Uses common GPT-4.1, tool and scoring interfaces, ten fixed benchmark sets and three runs. One of six multi-agent configurations beat the single-agent reference on aggregate; some configuration rankings reversed on particular tasks. [Fu et al., v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05670v2) | Counts of agents or successful sessions alone cannot identify improvement. Comparable model, contracts, task mix, evaluation and repeat runs make the observed outcomes interpretable. GPT-4.1-controlled results are not a ranking of today's offerings. |

## Evidence gaps and refresh

- **Quality and attribution.** No reviewed paper establishes the desired agent-version signal for this author's multi-repository environment. A source-backed finding is that memory utility, authorization, task quality and inference spend can diverge. A capability map should expose each dimension separately. Report actual model and configuration, protocol version, memory snapshot/citations, outcomes, denominator and scoring provenance if these are available. Comparison cohorts and independent evaluators are open evidence requirements, not validated design choices.
- **Fair curation comparison.** The sources measure varying things, including environment QA, next-session retrieval, selective forgetting, downstream success, cost, and negative transfer. The reports do not supply a common measure for source-preserving corrections, authorized cross-repository promotion, quarantine, intentional omissions, or post-restore evidence. A business-level curation claim should name which of those behaviors is hypothesized and which evidence actually covers it.
- **Stale work rechecked.** The 2026-05-12 LongMemEval-V2 record exposes no revised paper version as retrieved. Its environmental benchmark remains useful and is bounded by the newer AgentCL v3 and systems characterization v2 for transfer and resource questions. The 2026-01-12 [LLMRouterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07206v1) is an older per-query routing baseline; the materially newer stateful and per-subtask studies above limit its use for current workflows. Neither old benchmark is a current product capability source.
- **Warm leg and graph dedup.** Researched the theme's authorization measurement and cost/evaluation watchpoints, and searched Unimatrix as scout in technology and finding for AgentCL, Proactive Memory Agent, AuthorizationBench and routing. Prior entries #194 and #286 concern enforcement outside the principal, #202 and #205 identify existing policy and workload-identity mechanisms, and #230 is existing dogfood evidence about prefix-cache economics. The new studies extend measurement and interpretation. Count the routing-cache line as an alias of #230's mechanism, with a different experimental setting, and keep AuthorizationBench distinct from access-control implementations. LongMemEval-V2 is a different benchmark from the conversation benchmark and should not be counted as an independent vendor evaluation.
- **Protected cold leg and limits.** Primary-source searches in arXiv CS.AI, CS.CL and CS.CR for continual-learning control streams, history-aware router economics and authorization alignment found AgentCL, BenchAgent and the spreadsheet routing work outside the named vendors/watchlist. Published pricing and release features, patents, closed deployments and production identity are outside this surface. No reproduction or live behavioral experiment was performed.
