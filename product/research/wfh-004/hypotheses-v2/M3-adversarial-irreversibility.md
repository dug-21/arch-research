# M3 — Adversarial, failure-mode and irreversibility

**Run:** `wfh-004` · Issue #48 · phase `hypothesize` (rebuild) · read-only, zero graph writes. **31 candidates.**
**Read:** BRIEFING-v2 (full), W0-e §1+§7, W0-d2 §1–§3. Also verified two repo artifacts directly — see §Counterweight.

## 0. Position statement — read before the register

Both registers record **zero** adversarial, malicious, or compromised-agent incidents across 610 SDLC issues and 30 research pains.

- **22 of 31** rows are `field` — *the failure mode itself is demonstrated in our record, accidentally.*
- **9 of 31** are `reasoned` — no instance of either kind.
- **0 of 31** have field support for the **adversarial** instance. Not one.

> **That is the finding, not a caveat. Every failure in the field record is the accidental version of an attack. An adversary is not a new failure mode; it is a *selector* that picks the existing failure modes and fires them deliberately, in combination, at the moment they are least observable.** The registers already tell us what an attacker could do — they simply have not told us that anyone did.

**And why they have not: an environment with no detector reports no incidents by construction.** This repo has no CI at all; a merge with no gate trace was unnoticed until a census went looking; a record stopped for five weeks. *"Zero adversarial incidents" means zero adversarial detections by a system with no adversarial detector* — a statement about the instrument, not the environment.

**The frame for everything below:** every census-backed failure happened with CI green, and in the worst case the alarm returned PASS unconditionally and CI could not tell. **A competent adversary's actions all succeed. Our entire detection apparatus inspects *failures*. The demonstrated detection rate against successful actions is indistinguishable from zero in both domains — not low, *unmeasured*, because nothing samples successes at all.** That is M3-08, the row to defend hardest.

## 1. Candidate register

### Group A — authority, and the undifferentiated channel

**M3-01 — Content cannot authorize.** An instruction arriving inside content an agent read cannot cause a tool call the step was not already authorized to make; the refusal names the originating span. *Security · HARNESS ·* Authority is held by **the step, not the agent**; admission is a lookup outside the model against a set fixed *before* content entered context. Object-capability discipline: possession, not persuasion, authorizes. **F-17 is decisive that the *model* cannot supply this** — one undifferentiated channel, "not solvable at the model layer," expiring only if the architecture changes. **In:** D for admission; leaves *which action to request* on the model, and the span-naming must be **locator-verified** or the refusal message is F-11 self-report with no evidentiary weight. **Δ:** Sec↑ materially — the only structural answer to F-17. **Rel↓ slightly: rules fail closed on the unenumerated** — the regression is real and is why M3-29/30/31 exist. **Ev:** reasoned; field-adjacent S-10 (a written constraint overridden by the default spawn pattern — an instruction with no authority nonetheless steering execution). **Fa:** if authority sets must be so wide that essentially every call is admitted, the ability is ceremonial.

**M3-02 — Authority sealed at step start.** Authority cannot be widened by anything happening during the step; an attempted widening is a refused, recorded event. *Security · HARNESS · D ·* Monotonic non-increase — seccomp installed before untrusted input arrives. **The adversarial case: an agent that behaves for forty calls and requests one escalation at call forty-one, when attention has moved on. Without sealing, "authority" is whatever the last message argued for.** **Δ:** Rel neutral-to-down (a needed escalation now requires a new envelope — an argument for M3-29 being *fast*).

**M3-03 — Per-call context provenance.** For any tool call the harness can state which content spans were in context and each one's origin class. *Context/Security · HARNESS ·* **In: P → D** — pure reservoir: the information existed at injection and was discarded. Does not stop injection (M3-01 does); **makes forensics possible at all — today a compromise cannot be scoped even in principle.** Precondition for M3-18 and M3-26. **Ev:** reasoned for the attack; `field` for the gap — P-27/P-28, S-18.

**M3-04 — Narration vs record, separately addressable.** The agent's account and the harness's record are distinct, and divergence is a first-class recorded event. *Introspection · HARNESS · D ·* **Narration is F-11 — "silent by construction"; models verbalised a decisive hint 41%/19% of the time, and W0-e §7 marks the narrated/observed distinction durable across generations.** So the harness must never consume narration where a record exists. **The adversarial case: a deceptive agent's only lever over the record is its own narration; if narration is never authoritative, deception's payload has nowhere to land.** **Ev:** field — S-01, S-03 (44–79% store disagreement), P-13.

**M3-05 — Orphan-action detection.** Every action is attributable to a declared step that required it; an action with no requiring step is recorded. *Structure/Security · HARNESS · D ·* **The inverse of conformance:** conformance asks "did every declared step happen"; this asks *"did anything happen that no step declared"* — **the question that catches an agent doing extra work, and the question nobody asks.** An absence-detection over actions, which F-12 says the model cannot do but which is trivially decidable over a *set*. **Ev:** field — S-10, S-09 (26 unattributable processes), P-21 (14.8% of calls after completion).

### Group B — catastrophic vs loud; the observer's altitude

**M3-06 — The run as the observed object.** The harness reports a run that has stopped progressing without concluding, **independent of whether the underlying process is alive.** *Recovery/Introspection · HARNESS · D ·* **Process green, unit dead** — S-08: a maintenance tick dies, all maintenance stops forever, *"no observable indication,"* while the server keeps serving. **Adversarially the cheapest catastrophic move: you don't break anything, you make the thing that would have noticed look one level down.** **Ev:** field — S-08, S-07, P-10, P-26. **Fa:** if runs legitimately stall for long variable periods, the deadline is unsettable — **needs a probe on real run-duration distributions.**

**M3-07 — Negative control on every verdict mechanism.** Every mechanism that can emit pass/fail demonstrates on a schedule that it is **capable of emitting fail**, recorded with the same identity as real verdicts. *Introspection / proposed E-3 · HARNESS · D ·* **The field case is exact and devastating: the fix for the zombie-process bug shipped `setsid` without `-w`, so every failing suite and every timeout read PASS — "this inverts the bug it was meant to fix," and that bug's entire point was gate-signal integrity.** **An adversary does not need to defeat your gate; they need your gate to have been broken for a while — which ours was, by accident, with no detector.** **Do: both-differently — parameterization:** SDLC verdicts bottom out in an exit code, so the control is a fixture that must fail; research verdicts bottom out in a model call, so the control is a probe item with a known-wrong answer — **and because judge bias is systematic, a research negative control measures bias, not variance, and cannot be improved by sampling.** **Ev:** field — S-06, S-05, S-20.

**M3-08 — Coverage over *successful* actions.** The harness reports what fraction of **successful** actions were independently re-examined and by what — distinct from the failure-investigation rate. *Introspection / E-3 · HARNESS ·* **Audit sampling of clean transactions** — the practice of sampling normal operations rather than incidents, *because the population of incidents is selected by the detector and therefore tells you nothing about what the detector misses.* **The single most important row in this register, and it has no field support in either domain by construction.** Our whole apparatus inspects things that went visibly wrong; a competent adversary produces only successes. **Our measured detection power against a competent adversary is not low — it is *undefined*, because the denominator has never been sampled.** **The one time anyone looked at the success population (63 merged units), it immediately found 3–4 merges with no security or gate trace — ~5–6% on first look. That is the entire argument.** **In:** D for sampling/coverage; **I** for re-examining prose, **D** for code. **Δ:** Cost **up**, and it is real — sampling successes is pure overhead until it finds something. **Fa:** sample-size trap — a 5% defect rate needs a real sample before "nothing" means anything.

**M3-09 — Record-completeness verdict.** The harness reports gaps in its **own** records — a sequence that stopped, a period with no entries, a writer that went quiet. *Introspection / E-3 · HARNESS · D ·* Sequence identity makes a *missing* entry detectable **without knowing what it would have said.** **S-07 is five weeks long:** audit writes silently stopped, discovered 36 days later, *"no log line, no panic, no visible error to the caller."* **Adversarially: the cheapest way to defeat an audit log is not to forge entries — it is to stop it, because forgery is detectable and silence is not. Ours demonstrated a five-week silence tolerance with no adversary at all.** **Ev:** field — S-07, S-19 (142 of 1,585 scenarios dropped silently), S-24.

### Group C — the irreversibility register

**Preface: research has no irreversible operation at all.** Every row is one-domain-today and both-domains-tomorrow — **the SDLC evidence is doing double duty as the research domain's forecast.**

**M3-10 — Reversibility class stated before the act.** Before execution the harness states whether an action is reversible and by what means; an irreversible action cannot proceed without a **recorded pre-commitment that exists whether or not the action then succeeds.** *Recovery/Security · HARNESS; class table DEFINITION ·* Two-phase commit with a durable intent record. **The pre-record is load-bearing: after an irreversible act fails, the only evidence it was attempted may be external state you cannot read.** Also supplies the blast-radius statement — which one-way doors the current run is authorized to open. **Ev:** field — S-14 (two versions absent from the release list), S-13.

**M3-11 — Identity-keyed at-most-once.** A second attempt with the same identity is refused rather than performed. *Recovery · HARNESS · D ·* **F-21: the model cannot supply idempotent retry** (a retry is a fresh sample; >60% pass@1 → <25% pass^8), **so retry safety is a harness property or it does not exist.** Field case exact: `package-npm` is *not* idempotent. **Adversarially, a non-idempotent irreversible operation plus an agent that retries on error is a self-amplifying consumption of a scarce external resource — and our record shows five consecutive failed release runs.**

**M3-12 — Consumed-resource ledger including failures.** A one-way resource consumed by a **failed** action is recorded as consumed. *Recovery/Introspection · HARNESS · D ·* **The subtle and expensive case: failure consumed the resource anyway.** Two version numbers permanently unusable *because the publishes failed* — **a success-only record shows those versions as never used, which is precisely wrong and invites the retry that burns a third.** **Ev:** field (n=2 — genuinely thin; **a probe, not a build**).

**M3-13 — Write-path exclusion check at write time.** A write to a path feeding an irreversible publication channel is refused unless that path is recorded as excluded. *Security · HARNESS · D ·* **Write time is the whole point:** at commit time the credential is already on disk and may already have been read. **S-15: a live cloud bearer token in cleartext to a path verified *not* gitignored — *"a single `git add -A` commits a live credential into history."*** **Adversarially the highest-yield move in the register: silent, it *succeeds*, no error, and the exfiltration is performed by our own routine automation. An adversary does not need to transmit anything; they need our commit to do it.** **Do:** structurally one-domain but **both-identically in practice — the research repo commits to the same public git.** *(Flagging disagreement with W0-d2 §3, which reads this as SDLC-only: that is true of research operations, false of the research repo.)*

**M3-14 — Authority bound to the irreversible act.** The identity that permitted it is recorded at the moment of the act. *Security/Steering · HARNESS · D ·* Value is **scoping after the fact**, not deterrence. **Ev:** reasoned for the irreversible case; field for the general gap — S-18 (a limit moved *"at some point,"* provenance unrecoverable), S-21.

### Group D — attacking the record and the verifier

**M3-15 — Verdicts append-only with attempt identity.** A later verdict is recorded *alongside* rather than in place of the earlier one; the harness returns the full history including every superseded verdict. *Introspection/Structure · HARNESS · D ·* **The census is the material and it is damning: ≥53 failures overwritten in place, and one FAIL survives *only because a duplicate filing convention nobody standardised happened to preserve it*.** **An adversary attacking this record needs no privileged write access — they need to fail, then retry, which is the normal, encouraged, blameless path. The FAIL is destroyed by the mechanism that exists to let you fix it.** Second-order: **it also destroys the denominator for M3-08 and for every self-improvement measurement.** **Inc:** SDLC — **git *is* append-only, so this is the row where the substrate almost solves it and the convention defeats the substrate.**

**M3-16 — Closed verdict alphabet + designated location.** A verdict is consumable only if it is a declared value **in an artifact designated to carry verdicts**. *Structure/Introspection · DEFINITION → converted · D ·* Two field cases, one move: **you do not defeat the gate, you decline to be evaluated by it and emit something the consumer accepts anyway.** Exactly the class F-10 predicts — joint compliance falls below 50%, **terminal constraints drop first, and that is where gates live.** **The C-2 core is a *categorisation, not an essay*, and the harness never needs the verdict true — only attributable, bounded, recorded-before, re-checkable: exactly what M3-15+M3-16 supply.** **Δ:** Rel↓ — a legitimate novel verdict is rejected until the alphabet extends. **Direct argument for M3-29.**

**M3-17 — Authored, recorded injection from shared stores.** Injected content carries who wrote it and when; the injection event is recorded. *Context/Security · HARNESS · P→D ·* **Retrieved knowledge is auto-injected into every new specialist, so anyone who can write a node addresses every future agent, in both domains — a broadcast channel to all future execution with no author attribution at delivery.** Combined with F-17, **a single stored node is a persistent, self-delivering instruction to the entire future of the system.** The single-writer curator is the only thing between "anyone" and that channel — **and a single writer is a single point of total compromise.** **Inc:** the author identity on that channel is **currently, by design, `human` for every agent write** (the `agent_id` override). *The sharpest single sentence in this register.*

**M3-18 — Revocable influence / blast-radius query.** The harness can enumerate every run that consumed a stored item and mark those runs affected. *Context/Self-improvement/Security · HARNESS ·* **In:** D given M3-17; **I** for whether an affected run's *conclusion* actually changes. **Field support that this is not hypothetical: 52% of bugfixes name a prior internal unit as origin, four are bugfix-caused-bugfixes — contamination through our own artifacts is the *dominant* observed defect source, with no tracing mechanism.** **Inc:** SDLC partial and better than it looks — `git blame` + issue links gave a genuine trace **for code**, which is why 52% could be counted at all. **Nothing equivalent for knowledge.**

**M3-19 — Sensitivity-loss statement at change time.** When a check is changed such that it would no longer detect something it previously detected, the loss is reported **at the moment of the change**. *Self-improvement/E-3 · HARNESS · P ·* **In every field instance the loss *was* recorded — afterwards, in a separate issue, by someone who noticed. The information existed; it arrived too late to inform the decision.** **Adversarially, weakening a check is the ideal move: indistinguishable from ordinary maintenance, produces a green build, and its effect is felt only by the future.** **Do: both-differently** — SDLC checks are executable so the delta is measurable; research checks are prose criteria, so the delta is an agreement change **inheriting systematic bias — say so when reporting it.**

### Group E — multi-agent and insider shapes

**M3-20 — Independence reported as input-difference.** Two reviews are reported independent only when the harness can state their inputs differed, **and it names the inputs they shared.** *Introspection/Structure · HARNESS ·* **Two reviewers with identical inputs and priors are one reviewer sampled twice** — and **voting reduces variance, not bias** (a statistical fact, not a model fact). **So agreement between two same-kind readers is evidence of shared bias, not of correctness.** Field proof: a design reviewer approved a wrong diagnosis, a product reviewer *independently* approved it, **the human corrected it.** **In:** D for the input diff; **I** for whether differing inputs produce independent *judgment* — **the ability reports a lower bound on independence, not a measure of it.** **Δ:** Rel up *in the honest direction* — **it lowers apparent assurance to its true level, which will read as a regression to anyone who liked the old number.** **§9's shared-surface caveat applies this to this very run — including to M3.**

**M3-21 — Declared dual control.** For a declared class of writes, the performing and attesting identities must differ. *Security/Structure · DEFINITION → converted ·* **The single-writer rule is *correct* for auditability and *maximally concentrating* for compromise; those are the same property from two sides.** Dual control requires two failures instead of one. **Δ: Sec↑, Rel↓ — halves throughput on the constrained class and introduces a deadlock mode. A candidate that raises security and lowers reliability, declared per §8. It only pays on a narrow, high-consequence class, and expects to be narrowed hard at triage.**

**M3-22 — Spawned work owned and reaped.** Work started by an agent is attributable to it and does not outlive it. *Recovery/Security/Structure · HARNESS · D ·* **26 zombie processes from ≥6 sessions over twelve hours holding build and DB locks — and nobody could attribute them.** **Adversarially this is persistence: a process outliving its authorizing step is an unattributed agent holding live credentials on the machine.** Also the DoS shape — locks held against everyone else's work. **Cleanup must never be an instruction** (F-10: terminal constraints drop first, and cleanup is definitionally terminal).

**M3-23 — Two-sided spawn reconciliation.** A coordinator declares the roles it will spawn; the harness reports both a declared role that never ran **and a role that ran without being declared**. *Structure/Introspection · HARNESS · D ·* **Two-sided is the point.** Declared-not-run is field-proven at 3–4/63 and in the architecture revert. **The ran-not-declared side has no field instance and is the adversarial one: a coordinator that spawns everything is a single point of total compromise, and its compromise looks exactly like an extra helper role nobody declared. Nothing in either domain would notice.**

### Group F — cost, self-improvement, context, steering

**M3-24 — Metered, attributable, haltable spend.** *Cost · HARNESS ·* **Read the cost cell correctly: zero cost incidents in 610 issues is absence of record — nobody is trying to meter spend at all.** The research side has cost pains only because one run built an instrument, and **the first thing that instrument did was undercount by 61×.** **An adversary who simply makes work expensive is invisible by construction and needs no privilege at all** — a retry loop, a context-inflating document, a task that decomposes forever. **In:** D for metering/halting; **P** for attribution. **Takes off the model: counting (F-15 — no accumulator, wrong totals are fluent) and self-report of consumption. Leaves on it: nothing, and it should never have been on it.** **Δ:** Rel **mixed** — a halt is a new failure mode, and **a ceiling that only halts is a ceiling people will raise to infinity** (→ M3-30).

**M3-25 — Separation of the measured from the measurer.** Adoption on evidence records the evidence, the comparison, and the party that produced the outcome labels; adoption is refused where the label producer could also have altered the labels. *Self-improvement · HARNESS ·* **The corruption path for the whole reflexive loop: corrupt the outcome signal once and the system optimizes itself in the attacker's direction, permanently, while reporting improvement.** The field says the signal is already fragile without an adversary — a metric moved and could not be attributed across 5 candidate causes while silently dropping 9% of its input; 62/63 units carry no retro trace; **and ≥53 FAILs were overwritten, which means the outcome corpus self-improvement would learn from has already had its negative examples deleted by ordinary operation. A system that learns from a corpus with the failures removed will conclude it is doing well.**

**M3-26 — Reconstructable inputs, including the withheld set.** The inputs to any past step can be reconstructed **without asking the agent**, including what was deliberately withheld. *Context · HARNESS · P→D ·* **Controlling what an agent sees is the highest-leverage attack in the register precisely because it leaves no trace in either domain today** — the agent behaves correctly given its inputs, its reasoning is sound, its output is well-formed, and the manipulation lives entirely in a channel nobody records. **The withheld-set is the part that matters most and the part nobody builds: the only artifact that makes deliberate omission distinguishable from accidental omission after the fact.** **F-19 caveat: replay is not reproduction** — capturing inputs does not make output reproducible (80 distinct outputs at temp-0); **the ability is over *inputs* and must not be oversold.**

**M3-27 — Approval bound to the artifact version.** Any change after approval invalidates it. *Steering/Security · HARNESS · D ·* **The human gate is the most load-bearing control in every workflow definition we have — and the one control with no integrity binding at all.** Adversarial shapes all cheap: change the artifact after approval; **present a summary for approval and execute something else — F-7's omission asymmetry makes the summary a human approves the single most dangerous instance of the dangerous middle in the whole system, because a human is downstream of it and F-6 assumes the human is the checking layer**; reuse an old approval. **Inc:** SDLC partial and genuinely good (a PR approval is version-bound, stale reviews dismissable); research none. **One of the clearest "SDLC gets free, research must build" rows.** **Δ:** Rel↓ — churn re-invalidates approvals and irritates the approver into approving faster and looking less; **a real backfire M3-31 should watch for.**

**M3-28 — Bounded, consequence-ordered human decision queue.** *Steering · HARNESS ·* **An operator facing an undifferentiated stream of alerts responds to none of them — a property of operators, not of these operators.** The adversarial shape is a flood: many low-consequence requests degrade attention to the one high-consequence request, **and the human's own approvals become the attack's delivery mechanism.** No privilege, no forgery, looks like a chatty run. **The model must not rank its own requests' importance (F-14).** **Ev:** reasoned — and per §5 that is because **there is no mid-run steering to fail, so the absence is uninformative rather than reassuring.**

### Group G — the counterweight: controls that survive contact with their operators

Twenty-seven rows above make the harness stricter. **The consequence is not hypothetical and it is not in a register — it is in this repository, and it was verified directly:**

```
/home/vscode/.bashrc:163            alias dsp='claude --dangerously-skip-permissions'
/workspaces/arch-research/.devcontainer/postCreate.sh:32   echo "alias dsp='claude --dangerously-skip-permissions'"
```

> **The bypass is written by the environment provisioning script.** It is not a user's deviation from the intended setup; **it *is* the intended setup, installed on every fresh container, two keystrokes long.** That is the observable end-state of strictness without an appeal path: the control was not argued with, it was routed around, and the routing was then automated and checked into the repo.

**And S-11 reads the same way:** faced with a permission system that blocked spawned agents from writing, the shipped response **overrode `agent_id` to `"human"` on all calls**, trading the entire per-agent audit trail for throughput. **Both of our real encounters with an enforcement plane ended in an institutionalized bypass. Any candidate in this register lacking the three rows below should be assumed to end the same way.**

**M3-29 — Every refusal names an appeal authority and a response window.** *Steering/Security/E-4 · HARNESS ·* **A control is bypassed when the cost of compliance exceeds the cost of circumvention. `dsp` sets circumvention at two keystrokes. Nothing raises that; the only remaining lever is lowering the cost of compliance** — an appeal with a bounded response time is exactly that lever. **Rules fail closed on the unenumerated — *the* named cost of the determinism thesis — so an appeal path is not a softening of the thesis, it is the thesis's own required companion.** **Δ:** Sec **up, indirectly and strongly — it is what keeps the other 27 rows switched on. An exception path with a record is strictly stronger than a bypass alias with none.**

**M3-30 — Override with propagating provenance.** An override produces a record naming identity, control, reason, scope — **and that provenance travels with everything the override produced.** *Security/Introspection/E-4 · HARNESS ·* Break-glass. Three properties; **the third is always omitted:** (a) override is possible, so the control isn't routed around; (b) the record is append-only; **(c) provenance propagates to artifacts, so a result produced under override is not later consumed as though produced under the control.** Without (c) the record exists and nothing acts on it — **precisely S-11's outcome: *"no record of the trade travels with the writes."*** Complementary tier: a control that cannot be satisfied downgrades to record-and-proceed rather than blocking, **so the system has a response between "pass" and "stop."**

**M3-31 — Control-effectiveness telemetry.** Per control: refusal rate, override/bypass rate, false-refusal rate. *Self-improvement/Introspection/E-4 · HARNESS ·* **A control with a high override rate is a control failing its users, not users failing the control; a control that has never refused anything has never been tested and may be structurally unable to report red — which is why this row and M3-07 are the same concern from opposite ends.** **The bypass count is the one number that would have surfaced `dsp` as a *finding* rather than a line in a provisioning script nobody re-reads.** **Δ:** no regression **unless the numbers are used to weaken controls without M3-19's sensitivity statement — the two must ship together or this row becomes the Group-D attack with a dashboard.**

## 2. Failure catalogue — the pattern

27 scenarios. **The "what notices today" column reads *nothing* or *a human, by luck, later* in 24 of 27.** The three exceptions were noticed by a **one-off census, which is not a mechanism**. **Time-to-discovery for the successful-action failures is uniformly "never."**

Selected time-to-discovery: instruction-in-data → *never, the action succeeds* · gate structurally unable to report red → *unknown window* · record stopped → **~5 weeks** · FAIL overwritten by retry → **never; ≥53 lost, 1 survived by accident** · contamination from a prior internal unit → **52% of bugfixes** · spawned work outliving its agent → *~12h, recurring ≥3 releases* · cost inflated arbitrarily → **never; 0/610 records, 1 instrument off by 61×** · context shaped so a sound agent reaches a wrong conclusion → **never; invisible to the agent by F-18** · a control bypassed as routine → **never — the bypass is in the provisioning script.**

## 3. The irreversibility register

| Phase | Demand | Ability |
|---|---|---|
| Before | Know the door is one-way before touching it; blast radius enumerable | M3-10 |
| Before | A pre-commitment surviving the action's failure | M3-10 |
| Before | **The channel-adjacency check: a *reversible* write onto a path feeding an *irreversible* channel** | **M3-13 — the most under-rated row here: the dangerous act was writing a file** |
| During | At-most-once execution | M3-11 (**F-21: the model cannot supply this**) |
| During | Named authority bound to the moment | M3-14 |
| During | Dual control on the narrow high-consequence class | M3-21 |
| After | Consumed-resource ledger **including failed consumptions** | M3-12 |
| After | Resume rather than restart | M3-10 + M3-11 |
| After | Blast radius of a published artifact | M3-18 |

**Three observations the register forces:**

1. **The most consequential irreversible act in our record is not a publish — it is a file write.** The one-way door was `git add -A`, several steps downstream, operated by routine automation. **Irreversibility classification must follow the channel, not the syscall**, or it classifies `npm publish` and waves through the write that ends up in public history.
2. **Failure consumes the resource.** Two version numbers burned *by failing*. **Any register keyed on success is wrong at exactly the moment it matters.**
3. **Research's zero is a schedule, not a property.** The moment the harness gains a publish, deploy, email, or payment, research inherits all nine rows **with no incumbent whatsoever** — where SDLC at least has git, a rollback primitive, and PR review to build onto. **The domain with less exposure today has less machinery to survive gaining it.**

## 4. The silent-failure inventory — the compounding shape

Every dangerous-middle row plus every organizational silence. **W0-e marks 13 of 21 function classes silent**, and:

> **Silence at the model layer meets silence at the organizational layer — no run-level observer, no record-completeness check, no success sampling, no cost meter. Neither layer detects the other's failures, and the two silences are not independent: a run in which the model failed silently is also a run in which no organizational instrument was watching.** That composition is what turns a loud failure into a catastrophic one, and it is the mechanism behind *every census-backed failure happening with CI green.*

One entry with no remedy: **confidence.** Verbalized confidence tracks formatting, not correctness. **Do not consume it** — M3-28 explicitly refuses model-ranked urgency.

## 5. Coverage, distribution, emergent concerns

**Populated:** Structure (05, 15, 16, 23) · Context (03, 17, 26) · Security (01, 02, 13, 14, 17, 21, 30) · Introspection (04, 06, 08, 09, 15, 19, 31) · Cost (**24 only**) · Self-improvement (19, 25, 31) · Recovery (06, 10, 11, 12, 22) · Steering (27, 28, 29).

**The hole, and why this lens cannot see it — Human steering.** This lens works by taking a recorded failure and asking *"what if that were on purpose."* **Where there is no mechanism there is no failure record, so the method has no input.** M3-27/28 are the only rows generated with no accidental analogue anywhere. **A lens starting from the *desired* interaction rather than from failure would populate this concern far better.** *Secondary hole — Cost:* one row, because from an adversarial view the concern has one shape (unmetered work is unbounded work). **Writing four would be manufacturing candidates to fill a box.**

**Field/reasoned distribution — the deliverable:** **22/31 `field` · 9/31 `reasoned` · 0/31 with field support for the adversarial instance.**

> **How to read it: the 71% is not evidence that the attacker model has field support — it is evidence that the attack surface *is* the accident surface.** Every one of those 22 rows describes something that already happened here, unprompted, with no one trying. **An adversary contributes selection and timing, not novelty.** Stronger than "we imagined some attacks"; weaker than "we observed adversarial activity" — **and the 0% row exists so nobody conflates them.**

The 9 `reasoned` rows cluster in exactly two places — **architectural impossibilities** (01/02/03, resting on the strongest mechanism claims in the register) and **mechanisms we do not have** (27/28/29, resting on the weakest evidence). **Triage should treat them differently.**
