## 2. The context-quality argument (headline for charter a)

A **recorded** context answers "what went in." A **good** context is definable as five properties, each with an honest decidability status:

1. **Coverage** — it contains what bears on the task. Against a *declared* need-set: decidable (L6-11, closed). Against the *true* need: irreducibly open-world — **L6's irreducible core #2 stands and this register does not dispute it.** The retrieval-science move is not to decide sufficiency but to **bound and measure omission risk deterministically**: make the request space enumerable (-01), make omission require a correlated miss across independent selectors (-02), make every omission incident a permanent regression test (-09), and make what-the-corpus-cannot-answer a persistent record steering acquisition (-10).
2. **Allocation** — budget spent by declared value, not arrival order (-03); position spent where attention is (-06); knowledge staged to the phase that needs it (-07).
3. **Internal consistency** — no unflagged contradictions (-04): the relation-known half is pure computation over edges the corpus already stores; the free-text half is honestly I and unclaimed.
4. **Fidelity under compression** — composition stays I, accountability and measurement are D (-05). P-29 means compression is the garage's standing transport; its loss is currently a number nobody has.
5. **Relational integrity across units** — shared views provably shared, independent views provably independent (-11).

**P-28 worked directly.** The omission was invisible to the agent, and stays invisible under any pure-pull *or* pure-push design — that is L2-19's falsifier and it is correct. Three candidates answer it by different routes: **-01** is the direct counter (a one-line catalog entry would have made the absence visible *as an absence* — a known-unknown the unit's own leaf can chase); **-02** is the probabilistic counter (a graph-walk selector reaches the substrate node the interface-keyed semantic query missed); **-09** makes P-28 permanent (the corrected/uncorrected surface pair is a ready-made eval case with a measured 2× outcome delta).

**The decidable/inferential cut (C-2, A-3):** assembly, accounting, allocation policy, ordering, staging, structured-consistency, view-relation enforcement, and quality *measurement* are all D. The irreducible core is exactly two functions — open-world relevance and semantic-preservation judgment — both C-2 shells around forced-binary leaves. The addition round 1 lacked is a discipline point: **the I core's error rate is measurable and improvable deterministically (Cranfield) even though the judgment itself is never decidable.** Sufficiency-in-general remains inference; an omission-risk *discipline* is mostly computation.

## 3. The dynamic-shape argument (headline for charter b)

**Can a run's structure change and stay checkable? Yes — iff shape-change is an event, not an erosion.** The dilemma as posed (a rigid graph fights discovery; pure emergence is unobservable) assumes the declared shape is static. The third position is standard in the one domain that maintains continuous positive control over plans that change constantly — ATC: the flight plan is amended in flight, through the controller, never unilaterally, and every downstream party sees the current plan. Transposed (R2-3-16): at every instant there is exactly one current declared shape; work is checked against shape-as-of-now; mutations travel a recorded, authority-gated channel.

> **The invariant that makes an emergent run checkable is not "the shape doesn't change" but "the shape and the work never change independently."**

Two-tier authority keeps it C-4-clean: refinements within the declared envelope (A-3's sixth lens, inside an owner-set budget) are self-recorded; boundary-crossing changes (out-list, budget, role set) require a principal the run cannot write as — because a run authorizing its own boundary change is self-authorization, which is P-03 and L3 Group B exactly. The envelope is capturable structurally (L6-08's move), so the refinement-vs-boundary classification is mostly decidable.

The field record covers all three quadrants: legitimate change through the channel worked twice (A-3; wfh-002's W5 amendment); illegitimate change without it lost a run (P-03); rigid refusal was never the option anyone chose. The falsifier is friction — shape mutation retreating into prose — and its countermeasure is an interface property: **amendment must be cheaper than deviation**, which is testable.

**Which relations does research work actually need?** From the recorded runs, the relations *exercised in the field*: sequence, gate, **dependency** (-12), **fan-out/join** (-13), **iteration-with-stopping-rule** (-14), **composition-with-overrides** (-15), **amendment** (-16), and obligation (E-1). BPMN cargo *on present evidence*: authored XOR alternatives, event subprocesses, timer boundary events (except E-1's deadline use, L1-49), and compensation — mechanically clean (L1-39) but with zero field instances of a compensation ever executed; it stays Recovery's claim, not Structure's demand. And **-16 is the load-bearing candidate of this whole charter**: -12/-13/-14/-15 all carry the same falsifier (declared forms go stale on contact with research), and all four are rescued by the same amendment channel. The relations are the vocabulary; -16 is what lets the vocabulary survive contact.
