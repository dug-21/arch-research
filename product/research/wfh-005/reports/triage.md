### P3 — minimize inference; determinism is what makes a guarantee → **WOUNDED**

All four surfaces WOUNDED, and — this is the useful part — **they wounded it in three different places, none of which the position states.** The thesis survived a real attack. What did not survive is the operational lens derived from it, and that is the more expensive wound, because the lens is the sort key of wfh-004's entire register.

**What the attack confirmed.** Vendors put a model on the enforcement path and *decline the word guarantee for it*: gh-aw's own docs say "do not treat a 'safe' result as a security guarantee," publish no false-positive/false-negative rates, and title their troubleshooting section "AI detection always fails"; PINT puts the whole classifier tier at 89–95% with a published evasion study. AWS markets deterministic boundary evaluation verbatim as the product benefit. **Nobody ships inference as the envelope** (W3). That is strong corroboration of the core claim and it should be recorded as such.

**The three narrowings, merged into one statement:**

> A guarantee requires that the **deciding** step be deterministic, external to the model's context, and **monotone** — able to narrow without approval, never to widen. Inference is permitted, and often necessary, on the **proposing** and **authoring** sides, provided a deterministic checker sits downstream at the gate's altitude. The binding constraints on a deployed guarantee are therefore **(a)** whether any model call sits on the *deciding* leg, **(b)** whether the decision is monotone, **(c)** the size of the **exemption surface** within the operator's reach, and **(d)** whether the residual friction fits inside the operator's **compliance budget**. The count of model calls is not one of them.

Sources for each: (a) DO-330's tool-qualification criterion — an unassured tool is permitted on the path precisely when its output is independently verified — which is **prior art against us**, plus Progent, CaMeL and proof-carrying generation (W1, W4). (b) Progent's SMT-checked monotonic confinement (W1). (c) Every deterministic-enforcement product in the scan ships a first-class bypass because rollout without one fails: `LOG_ONLY`, `dryrun`/`warn`, Gatekeeper's exempt annotation, `Audit`, `threat-detection: false`, gh-aw issue **#29171** requesting a runtime bypass with a business justification, and the permission-bypass mode (W2, W3). (d) The compliance budget (NSPW 2008), rational rejection of security advice (Herley 2009), shadow security (2014), normalization of deviance, practical drift, drift to danger, and approval fatigue measured across 21 production systems (W1, W4).

**Where I think a scout overreached — say it plainly.** W4 cites **DORA** as the measured verdict that "the deterministic gate is net-negative." DORA measures *external human change approval by a change manager or CAB*: a high-latency, human-in-the-loop gate. The theme's mechanisms are mostly **automatic and zero-latency** — a compile-time permission derivation costs no lead time at all. Using DORA against determinism is an altitude error and it overstates the case. **The compliance-budget and normalization-of-deviance arguments survive intact and are sufficient; the DORA transplant is weaker than W4 presents it and should not be cited at the gate as a measurement of our mechanism.** W4 similarly flagged its own 70%-BPM-failure and iBPMS figures as practitioner/vendor claims — those flags should be honoured.

**What W3 saw that nobody else did, and it is the sharpest thing on this position.** Anthropic's response to widespread use of the permission-bypass mode was not to harden the deterministic gate; it was to **put a model classifier on the path in order to remove the friction that caused the bypass**. Inference is being deployed, deliberately, as a *friction reducer inside a deterministic envelope, so that the deterministic envelope stays switched on*. The theme has no position on that pattern, and it is the one the field converged to.

**Two requirements are absent from wfh-004's register entirely and both are load-bearing:** monotonicity of the enforcement decision, and a bypass-resistance / compliance-budget criterion. A shortlist that cannot express *"this gate will be turned off in week three"* is sorted by the wrong key even after the determinism question is settled.

**BUILD — exactly one thing.**

*Required field — the specific thing assembly cannot deliver:*

> **Deriving and enforcing an authority bound for a principal whose demand set is discovered during execution rather than declared before it — monotonically, such that the bound can narrow without approval and can never widen without one — and enforced by a plane the principal holds no credential for.**

Nothing on the shelf does this. The two closest existing patterns both derive from **observation** rather than declaration — IAM Access Analyzer (CloudTrail activity → least-privilege policy, GA 2021) and `audit2rbac` — which is exactly the input an LLM agent *can* supply; and Progent supplies the monotone half with an SMT solver deciding narrow-versus-widen. That those two halves exist separately and have never been composed for a non-declarative actor is the honest statement of what would be built.

*Proposed `done_when` (advisory, the owner charters it):* given one real run in this repo whose demand set was **not** declared in advance, the system derives a bound from observed demand, and a subsequent attempt to exceed that bound is **refused by a plane the agent holds no credential for**, with the refusal attested. Artifact-producing, bounded, and testable in days.

*What would kill this BUILD, stated because the burden of proof is on building:* a positive finding in either unread cell from §1 — commercial CD platforms binding `(pipeline, stage, role)` as one derived object, or one of the five unread USPTO grants covering automated least-privilege derivation. **Both sit under this recommendation and both were left unread.** Close them before chartering.

### P6 — one statement

> **Every mechanism in this theme takes as input a multi-phase workflow definition that is authored before the run, reused across runs, and written by a principal the executor cannot be — and in this operation it is authored per run, revised mid-run, and written by the same party that executes and grades it; which makes "deriving the capability set from the phase" hand-writing the ceiling one level up and renamed, and makes phase-indexed authority self-restriction by the credential holder.**

W4's second position and W2's independent observation (gh-aw's self-improvement hands a model write access to the artifact every ceiling derives from) supply the third clause: in every adjacent field without exception, the policy author sits outside the executor.

**Would I put it in front of the owner ahead of the five? It is also the **cheapest** thing in the run to decide: one hour, no spawn.

**And here is the skeptical counter, which the owner must hear alongside it.** P6's strongest evidence is about **the wrong population.** W1's 20,574 sessions measure *developers using coding agents interactively*; the theme targets *authored, durable multi-agent protocols*, which this garage demonstrably writes. W1 flags this itself: *"our own operation is an unusual case — we do author durable multi-phase protocols."* W4's evidence is a field analogy, not a measurement of us. And **the leader's spot-check is explicitly preliminary and explicitly not a measurement** — its stated limits are that the deviation scan keyed on a formatting convention only wfh-005 uses (undercounting every other run) and that protocol-file commit counts measure method churn, not per-run phase-graph reuse. **I rely on it for direction only, and I say so: three signals all consistent with P6, none of them measuring it.** Magnitude is not established. That is precisely why the falsifier should run before the owner rules — not because P6 is established, but because it is cheap and load-bearing.

**Cheapest falsifier — one hour, no spawn, two counts.** W4's probe, sharpened, because "unchanged" is too strict a test:
