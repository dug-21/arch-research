## 1.3 My derived failure axes

The five-item contract does not exist, so these are mine, each with the cases behind it.
**⚠ Axes A, B and C are corrected by Amendment 1 — read it before citing them.**

### Axis A — Third-party authorship never started. The wall is at domain 1.

**Cases:** Chandler (11 plugins, 0 third-party, 6 years) · Genode (20 depot namespaces, 0 third-party, ~20 years) · Thingpedia (45 founding devices from a class assignment; 22/23 authors Stanford; 7 devices ever reached `universe` in 8 years) · CALO/RADAR (0%) · Urbit (~20 maintained apps, grant-funded in address space; 501 course signups → 49 contributors) · Solid (40 catalog apps under a no-testing disclaimer, insider says most don't work) · Mycroft (37/78 curated = core team) · Humane (zero, ever).

The framing presumes a growth curve — *domain 1, 2, … 6, where does it break?* **None of these systems ever entered that curve.** The measurable quantity is not "how many domains before the wall" but "did anyone outside the core team ever author one," and the answer is approximately zero from the beginning. **Whatever this run's criticality report ranks, it should rank against the count of domains the owner can personally staff, because that is the count every prior attempt actually achieved.**

### Axis B — There are two governance layers with opposite empirical signs, and the framing names them as one

This is the axis I'd most want carried to triage.

- **A runtime permission/capability model** — what an extension may *do*. **Anti-correlates with author supply.** Almond's TACL compiled to SMT formulas → ~1 non-Stanford author. Mycroft's `exec_module` at full user privilege → 454 authors. Genode's capability delegation, architecturally superb → 0 outside publishers. seL4, formally verified → no personal OS at all.
- **A publishing contract** — namespace, review, distribution channel, fork policy. **Correlates positively with an ecosystem existing at all.** Obsidian's developer policy → 6,685 plugins. Chandler's absence of any third-party contract → 11, all first-party. Urbit in 2021, eight years in: *"the biggest hurdle to getting more apps on Urbit is that there's no real way to distribute them."*

The framing's **capability vocabulary** is the first kind. Its **shell / init and supervision** is closer to the second. **They must not be scored on one axis** — the evidence says tightening one suppresses supply while the other is a precondition for supply existing. `[inferred]` on causal direction in both cases; the correlations are sourced.

### Axis C — `/etc` is load-bearing, but the load-bearing property is custody and schema-evolution authority, not format

Two sub-failures, both well-evidenced.

**(i) Schema agreement.** Solid diagnosed it in 2019 (Verborgh: RDF alone "does not guarantee interoperability"; fix = shapes), formalized it in 2022 (Dedecker et al.: address-book app and birthday app impose *conflicting, individually-correct* structures), and the fix is **still v0.2 draft in July 2026**. Anytype hit the identical wall from the other side and is rebuilding as Collections 2.0 — *"Why should an object only have one type?"* Urbit dodged it by having no schema layer and paid in agent coupling and state migration. Chandler's universal-item model made every feature a schema question and the schema was never settled.

**The decisive variable is who may evolve the schema.** Anytype can fix it on a 2-month cadence because it owns client, server, protocol and schema. Solid cannot, because its fix needs consensus across independent implementers. *Centralized authorship of the extension model is a liability for sovereignty and an asset for schema evolution* — and every attempt that optimized for sovereignty paid for it in schema paralysis.

**(ii) Custody.** Chumby (server-side playlist → one clock face) · Wave (provider XML, PDF export) · Mycroft (Selene died with the company; OVOS names *this*, not the patent troll, as the fault) · Rabbit (no delete path) · Limitless (14-day region-by-region erasure on acquisition) · mem0 (an LLM deletes what it judges contradictory). **Counter-case:** Plan 9's namespace is built by the user's own login script, no vendor holds it, and it is the one piece that survived — into Linux namespaces, containers, WSL, Crostini, QEMU.

### Axis D — The secret broker is an extensibility component, not a security component

**Cases:** Almond — five artifacts per domain, one of which (the OAuth app registration) is *an account relationship with a third party that a contributor structurally cannot author*; that is the mechanism that capped Thingpedia at 22-of-23-Stanford. Mycroft — the vendor's cloud *was* the credential store, so it died with the vendor. Magic Cap — $200M and 16 partners, killed because the only Place the agents could reach was AT&T's PersonaLink and AT&T switched it off. Zapier's counter-move — partner-built integrations, so the integrating *service* holds the credential relationship, and the count is 9,958+.

**Restated as a rule with evidence behind it: whoever must hold the credential relationship for domain N+1 determines who is able to author domain N+1.** If that party is you, you author all of them, and the count stops at what your team can staff. This is the single most direct piece of evidence this surface produced about a named component in the framing, and it reframes that component's job.

### Axis E — The extension unit shipped years late in every failed case

Urbit: no real app distribution as of 2021 (8 yrs); non-Hoon languages June 2026 (13 yrs). Solid: interop spec v0.2, 2026. Anytype: SDK "In Exploration," 2026 (8 yrs). Chandler: 1.0 at 6 yrs, two parcel namespaces. **The contrast is Mycroft — it had a working skill format and a review process early, and got 454 outside authors.** It then died of litigation, not of extensibility. `[inferred]` If a fourth attempt is contemplated, the extension unit is empirically the hardest thing to get right and the latest to arrive in every prior case.

### Axis F — Incumbent absorption is now the dominant ending

Ecco Pro → Outlook bundling (killed at peak reputation, vendor CEO on record). Mycroft's voice stack → HA Assist by default, same months. Rewind/Limitless → Meta (2025-12-05), founder on record about why. ## 2.2 The verdict, plainly

**Partly solved — and the honest answer is an ADOPT with a named artifact for three of the four properties, plus one property that is genuinely open.**

| Property | Solved? | The name, and what you'd adopt |
|---|---|---|
| Append-only, enforced rather than promised | **Solved** | **Transparency log** — Merkle tree + signed tree heads + inclusion/consistency proofs. Adopt **Sigstore Rekor** (use it, don't run it); lineage CT / RFC 6962 / Trillian / Tessera |
| Declared-kind evidence, cryptographically bound | **Solved, in one vertical, and the name is *attestation*** | **in-toto Attestation Framework v1** + **DSSE**. The declared kind *is* `predicateType`, a required URI, authenticated alongside the payload by PAE. **VSA and SVR are ready-made "we adjudicated this" predicates** |
| Evidence kinds **ranked**, with an insufficiency floor | **Solved — but only outside computing, and this is the best hit in the scan** | **PCAOB AS 1105 .08, ISA 500 A35, AS 2201 .50.** A **lattice with comparative operators, a stated floor, seven named procedure kinds, and a contradiction-resolution rule.** Free, stable, and no computing artifact has an equivalent. Carry its caveat: ISA 500 A35 states the ranking is **defeasible** |
| Defined asserter set, authority graded to the claim | **Solved in regulated fields, absent in computing** | **14 CFR 43.7/43.9(a)** — the licence class is a *field in the record*. **EU GMP Annex 11 cl.15** is the one place a regulation requires the *software* to enforce who may commit. in-toto layouts give `pubkeys` + `threshold`, not qualification |
| **Bitemporality — "when did we believe it vs when was it true"** | **Genuinely open in combination** | Owned by **Datomic / XTDB / SQL:2011**. **The two families have never been joined in a shipped product. Rekor v2 removed even integrated time.** `[inferred]` the most defensible gap |

**Free additions worth stealing:** the aviation **MEL** / nuclear **LCO** typed time-boxed exception register; **EASA M.A.801(d)**'s provisional commit recording the maker's qualifications inline and requiring ratification within seven days; **145.A.50(e)**'s release that must declare its own incompleteness on its face; **Datomic's** un-excisable excision records; and FDA 820.30(f)'s rule that a commit points at the prior claim it discharges.

**Where it stops:** (1) **artifact-shaped, not claim-shaped** — in-toto subjects are digest-matched, and our commits are about propositions; (2) **nothing enforces "no evidence ⇒ no commitment"**; (3) **declared-kind *checking* is human everywhere except in-toto and SAF-T**; (4) **the enforcement machinery in every regulated field is a subpoena** — strip the inspector and what remains is a well-shaped log with no teeth; (5) **the primitive checks the shape of the commit, never the truth of the evidence** — Wirecard: every invariant held, debits equalled credits, and €1.9bn did not exist because a forged bank confirmation was accepted on its face.

**The cost signal:** FDA priced Part 11 at "no net costs" then suspended enforcement of its audit-trail clause; FDA deleted design-review independence on 2026-02-02; SOX 404(b) was means-tested by exemption; ICH E6(R3) dropped the phrase "source data verification" entirely; FRE 902(13)/(14) replaced a witness with paper; AWS withdrew QLDB; ELN adoption sits at ~11% against >70% demand. **Six regimes, all reducing this primitive on cost grounds.** The one counter-signal: **the only field with no authority model — scientific workflow provenance — is the only one that failed.**

## 3.1 Suspected cross-surface aliases — flag only, I cannot see the other scouts

- **"evidence-graded commit"** ≡ *attestation / `predicateType`* (S1/S3) ≡ *sufficient appropriate audit evidence* (this surface) ≡ *ALCOA+ / audit trail* ≡ *bitemporal as-of* (S2) ≡ *Define-XML origin*. **Five names, one object.**
