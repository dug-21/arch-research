# hypotheses — H-D · composition without consolidation

**Run:** `wfh-007` · Issue #64 · `wf-v0.24` · hypothesizer H-D · divergent phase
**Partition:** how independently-owned programs, repos, workflows, skills, and memory instances compose into a growing personal digital organization without collapsing ownership boundaries (OWNER-DIRECTION §1, §4, §8, §10).
**Firewall:** every entry is an unproven conjecture, `claimed` at most. Nothing here grades, decides, or recommends. Where an entry names an artifact that would have to be *authored* (schema, namespace, protocol), that is flagged as the architecture-scope gap per §13.10 — described, not designed.
**Note:** this is H-D's revised set, incorporating the leader's mid-flight live-graph addendum, which upgraded H13 and H15 from conjecture to worked-example-grounded and seeded H19.
**Input quality:** not thin. Densest inputs: S4 + Amendment 1, the merge file, OWNER-DIRECTION, and the leader's addendum (229 active nodes; the `uni` delivery corpus unreachable from this slug; workflow ≠ knowledge ratified and enforced). The Unimatrix repo's `.claude/protocols/uni` corpus is **not on this disk**; hypotheses depending on its contents say so.

**Axis B applicability — stated once, referenced throughout.** The author-supply arm (runtime permission model anti-correlates with third-party authors) does **not bind** a single-operator system in years 1–2: there is no third-party author supply to suppress. Three arms **do** bind even at N-of-1: (a) the **retrofit cost** — HA recruited 19.7% of its catalogue in 21 months after a governance reset, gold = 13, so governance is present at capability 1 or recruits a fifth of capability N; (b) the **reviewer-throughput ceiling** (Cluster G) — the owner is the only reviewer, so it binds *harder* at N-of-1; (c) the **publishing-contract arm**, because the relevant "third parties" arrive early anyway — as model-authored extensions (Cloudflare OS's explicit bet, Umbrel's June 2026 pivot), not humans.

## Cluster I — Identity and namespace

### H1 — Global names only at the crossing; everything else stays local
- **Statement:** A federated naming discipline could require global uniqueness *only* for names appearing inside a cross-program contract (requesting program, contract id, workflow instance, disclosed-resource refs), leaving all intra-program names permanently local — because the only demonstrated survivor among config/namespace designs is the one where the view is assembled locally and no central party holds it.
- **Mechanism:** Plan 9's per-process namespace is the one design in S4's cohort that survived by propagation (Linux namespaces, containers, WSL, Crostini) — it scaled *down* and never needed a global registry. Per-slug Unimatrix isolation (proven, #12) is the same shape already running: local ids never resolve outside their slug and nothing has broken. The contract is the only surface where two namespaces meet.
- **Falsifier:** Run the §7 round trip and log every name the *accepting* side must resolve. If acceptance ever needs to dereference a name that stayed local, the minimal-global set is wrong and the boundary leaks namespace pressure inward.
- **Novelty:** adjacent. **Attention:** near-zero standing cost — no registry to maintain.

### H2 — Trust graduation forces principal durability at exactly one grain, and that grain is the program
- **Statement:** §5.7 (twenty successful runs *in the same action and consequence class*) could resolve §8 Q1 — stable principal vs per-objective team — by mechanism rather than taste: the 20-run record must accumulate against *something durable*, and the only entity durable across objectives is the program (repo + credential relationships), so teams can be assembled per objective *provided* their run-records roll up to a durable program principal.
- **Mechanism:** in-toto layouts show per-run functionaries work fine for *execution*; 14 CFR 43.9(a) shows the converse — where authority is graded to a claim, the certificate is a *field in the record*. Trust accounting is the 43.9 case: "20 eligible successes" is meaningless unless the identity persists across those 20 runs. Ephemeral teams + durable program satisfies both owner needs.
- **Falsifier:** Construct a consequence class whose 20-run record naturally spans *two* programs (e.g. "file an Issue on an external repo," done by both delivery and garage). If the owner's intuition says those runs pool, the durable grain is the *action class*, not the program.
- **Novelty:** non-obvious — derives an identity-architecture constraint from a trust-policy clause nobody connected to it. **Attention:** zero new gates; makes the existing §5.7 gate *computable*.

### H3 — Caller-asserted identity is already failing silently; substrate-minted identity is the correction
- **Statement:** SPIFFE-shaped workload identity (#205) could replace caller-asserted `agent_id` — because the live dogfood system demonstrates the failure today: every factory agent passes `agent_id` and every write records `created_by: anonymous`. Assertion-based identity does not fail loudly; it fails by recording nothing, the worst mode for a system whose trust graduation depends on per-actor records (H2).
- **Mechanism:** D6's own caveat is a running counterexample to identity-by-assertion. AIP sharpens it: *audit evasion via empty context* was caught only by the chained model — only when identity is cryptographically bound into the invocation.
- **Falsifier:** When the platform agent registry lands, check whether registry-asserted (still self-reported) identity suffices for the §5.7 record, or whether an actor can still run under a peer's id. If self-report + registry is enough for a single-owner threat model, minting is over-engineering for slice 1.
- **Novelty:** obvious (the evidence is our own). **Attention:** zero owner gates.

## Cluster II — Delegation across an ownership boundary

### H4 — The contract's "authority delegated" field is a token chain, not a description of one
- **Statement:** IBCT-style chained tokens (Biscuit + Datalog) could make the *authority-delegated* field self-enforcing: the root block declares scopes, budget ceiling, max delegation depth, expiry; every hop across an ownership boundary appends a block that can only narrow, widening rejected cryptographically at 0.049 ms — so the owner's leaning toward narrow-derived grants becomes structural rather than a convention every agent must remember.
- **Mechanism:** per-hop monotone attenuation enforced by verification, not reviewer vigilance; AIP's 600-attempt run caught delegation-depth violation only in chained mode. The budget-ceiling field maps onto §5.4 and §8's budget/expiry/cancellation fields.
- **Falsifier:** two-sided. (a) Provenance: single author, no venue — reproduce the reference implementation. (b) Fit: run one contract where the *legitimate* return requires authority the request chain lacks. If legitimate composition routinely needs escalation beyond the chain, monotone narrowing is the wrong default and the owner becomes the escalation hot path.
- **Novelty:** obvious. **Attention:** *reduces* gates — attenuation violations become verification failures, not review findings.

### H5 — Program-class grants revoked by version bump, not by enumeration
- **Statement:** Matter's CAT pattern — a versioned tag granting a whole *class*, revoked by re-issuing at a new version — could make "revoke everything the research garage may currently do" one version write, not a sweep over every resource ACL in every repo and instance.
- **Mechanism:** the version lives in the credential, the check at the resource; no per-resource write on revocation. The only shipped revocation primitive in the scan whose cost is independent of both resource count and actor velocity — which matters because S3's velocity result says TTL is the wrong instrument for a continuously-running fleet.
- **Falsifier:** S2's inertness flag — most Matter controllers write one Administer entry and never use the mechanism. Test whether class-grants stay exercised or degrade to one all-powerful grant per program; Cluster H is the named graveyard for exactly this.
- **Novelty:** adjacent. **Attention:** one owner action revokes a class; the inverse risk is a grant so coarse the owner never inspects it.

### H6 — The Fiat containment rule, pointed at the return path
- **Statement:** Spinnaker's shipped invariant ("only users with every role the service account has may use it") could govern acceptance (§8 Q3) by inversion: a *return* may not confer on the requester any effect-authority beyond the original contract's ceiling — acceptance becomes a deterministic check of the return against the request's declared deliverable and authority envelope, confused-deputy-resistant without a human gate.
- **Mechanism:** the production-CD precedent shows the rule is enforceable as a set-comparison at invocation time. Cluster D's cost evidence points the same way — ~370 data points per error found is the price of *human* acceptance review.
- **Falsifier:** the garage's most valuable returns may be precisely the *unrequested* findings — this run's Cluster G was emergent, not requested. If deterministic acceptance rejects or strands surplus, the check destroys the value the garage exists to produce. Test with this run's own outputs as the sample.
- **Novelty:** adjacent. **Attention:** removes the owner from routine acceptance; owner reviews only escalations and surplus.

### H7 — Ownership boundaries as a (partial) aggregation control
- **Statement:** The deliberate three-repo separation could defend against aggregation (Denning, 40 years unsolved, renamed twice in 2026) by making cross-program crossings *rare, typed, and contract-mediated*: the trajectory that matters is compressed into an auditable sequence of exchanges at the boundary, whereas consolidation makes every action same-plane and the trajectory unobservable.
- **Mechanism:** per-action authorization cannot see aggregation; trajectory assurance needs a bounded observation surface. A federation whose only inter-program channel is a contract log gives the trajectory a place to *be* — the exchange history is small (today: exactly one Issue), typed, reviewable at human throughput. Not a solution; a claim that the boundary topology reduces the surface where it must be watched.
- **Falsifier:** construct an aggregation attack composed entirely of within-boundary actions plus individually-innocuous contract fields. If the choke point sees nothing, the boundary gives false comfort — worse than no comfort (Cluster H).
- **Novelty:** non-obvious/whitespace — turns the owner's constraint from a preference into a candidate *security property*. **Attention:** trajectory review is human-affordable only because crossings are rare; at 100× contract volume this reverts to unwatchable.

### H8 — Consequence class does not survive the crossing; the contract must re-derive it
- **Statement:** Revisable-by-Design's proof that costs are properties of the *action space* implies a non-obvious contract rule: a consequence classification assigned in the caller's action space is *not valid* in the callee's — a garage finding is "reversible" inside its instance (supersession exists) but potentially "irreversible" once the delivery team acts on it externally. The contract carries the *material*; each side classifies against its own action space.
- **Mechanism:** the impossibility result is indexed to the action space by construction; two programs with different tools have different action spaces by definition. Cluster B's five shipped instances all classify at the point of *execution*, never at the point of *request* — the field agrees with the theorem.
- **Falsifier:** enumerate the §7 round trip's crossings. If every crossing in slice 1 is information-only (no delegated effects), re-derivation is moot for now and this is a domain-2 problem — itself worth knowing before the schema is authored.
- **Novelty:** non-obvious. **Attention:** zero new gates; relocates one classification step to the side that can perform it.

## Cluster III — Information flow between memory instances

### H9 — Classification survives transport only if authenticated *with* the payload
- **Statement:** DSSE's PAE construction — `predicateType` authenticated jointly with the payload so the type cannot be swapped to reinterpret the same bytes — could apply the same move to sensitivity: the classification label and onward-disclosure rule are signed *with* the disclosed material, so a label cannot be stripped or downgraded in transit through Issues, queues, or future channels, no matter how dumb the transport.
- **Mechanism:** GitHub Issue bodies are silently mutable by anyone with triage access; a label carried as Issue metadata has no integrity. PAE binding makes tampering *detectable*, converting the transport question from "trusted channel?" to "verified envelope?" — the property that lets Issues stay the human-visible surface without becoming the semantic protocol.
- **Falsifier:** post one signed contract through a real Issue round trip including a human edit of the body. If operational reality (agents quoting, humans reformatting, GitHub normalizing whitespace) breaks signatures constantly, the envelope is unusable on this transport and classification must ride a side channel — a materially different architecture.
- **Novelty:** adjacent. **Attention:** zero owner gates; verification failures replace a class of silent leaks.

### H10 — Who classifies: export-time classification is the curator role at the boundary
- **Statement:** The single-writer curator discipline could extend to information flow: the source instance's curator is the classification authority for anything leaving its instance, classifying at *export time* — one accountable classifier per boundary, matching the existing one-writer-per-graph rule and matching who actually knows the material's provenance.
- **Mechanism:** classification requires knowing where knowledge came from and what it touches — precisely the metadata the curator already handles (`cites:`, grades, tags). PCAOB's nested structure supports the shape: asserter and attester are distinct roles, and the attestation is not a separate engagement.
- **Falsifier:** §6 insists the five axes are separate; sensitivity classification of health or finance material may need domain judgment the curator lacks. Hand a curator ten mixed entries and score misclassification. A meaningful error rate on sensitive classes routes classification to the owner and the attention cost explodes.
- **Novelty:** obvious. **Attention:** the load-bearing question — this is exactly a bet that classification can be delegated at a tolerable error rate; its falsifier is the owner's gate count.

### H11 — §6's five failure axes already have five separate shipped carriers; the only unauthored artifact is the contract schema
- **Statement:** The refusal of a single `dangerous` flag could be satisfied by composition rather than construction: resource authority → attenuated token chains (H4); information sensitivity → PAE-bound labels (H9); reversibility/consequence → per-action-space classification (H8); evidence quality → kinded ordinal grades (the garage's own `grade:` tags, backed by the PCAOB lattice and the Graydon–Holloway negative against numeric scores); external representation → an owner gate with no mechanical substitute (§5.5, boundary undefined by the owner's own statement). Five axes, five carriers, all adopt-shaped — the one thing that must be *authored* is the contract schema that composes them, which is precisely the §13.10 architecture-scope gap, named and not designed.
- **Mechanism:** each mapping is to a mechanism with shipped or formally characterized instances. The composition claim is the conjecture; the components are not.
- **Falsifier:** find a §6 failure class that falls *between* carriers. Candidate: "misleading the owner through hallucinated information" is an evidence-quality failure only if hallucination reliably manifests as an evidence-kind defect — a fabricated `cites:` entry with a plausible ref passes kind-checking and fails only on dereference. If the gap is real, a sixth carrier (reference dereferencing / spot-audit) is missing.
- **Novelty:** adjacent. **Attention:** concentrates owner attention on axis five, the one the owner already said needs definition.

## Cluster IV — Second-brain elevation (§10)

### H12 — Elevation is an attestation about a finding, not a copy of one
- **Statement:** The PCAOB nested structure could type the elevated entry as a *new finding in the owner's instance about the project finding*: carrying the elevation judgment (provenance checked, sensitivity classed, contradictions reviewed), an evidence-reliability ranking from the AS 1105 lattice (independent-external > internal-with-controls > obtained-directly > inference, with the inquiry-alone floor), and a `cites:` reference to its origin — rather than a replicated payload pretending to be native knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** the lattice solves §10's hardest open point — *how evidence grades translate across projects with different ontologies* — by not translating them: the elevated entry's grade is the reviewer's ranked assessment of the source evidence's *kind*, which is ontology-independent. The defeasibility caveat matches the owner's contradiction-review step. `cites:`-as-field is the repo's own existing seam for reference-across-a-boundary-without-an-edge.
- **Falsifier:** an attestation without the payload may be useless at retrieval time — the second brain's value is answering questions, and an entry saying "project X proved something about Y, reliability high, see origin" forces a dereference that per-slug isolation currently forbids (H13). If retrieval-utility testing shows attestation-only entries are dead weight, elevation must copy at least a summary payload, and the corruption/exposure surface returns.
- **Novelty:** non-obvious. **Attention:** one owner gate per elevation initially, with H14 as the amortization path.

### H13 — Per-slug isolation makes elevation-by-reference structurally impossible today; the copy/reference choice is really a choice about building a read channel
- **Statement:** Proven per-slug isolation plus the leader's live verification (the `uni` corpus unreachable from this slug *by design*) hardens the §10 decision into a trilemma: elevated knowledge is (a) **copied** — payload duplicated, origin recorded but not dereferenceable, source-corrections invisible; (b) **referenced** — requires a cross-slug read channel that does not exist and that weakens the isolation just proven; or (c) **published** — the source instance *pushes* to a separate owner-controlled instance through the same governed contract machinery as any other cross-program exchange, making elevation literally a special case of the §8 work contract rather than a new memory feature.
- **Mechanism:** option (c) is the structurally distinctive one: §10's pipeline (nominate → check → classify → review → authorize → retain origin) is field-for-field a sub-schema of §8's contract field list. **Two open product questions may be one object.** Version-pinned id semantics (`context_correct` reissues ids; stale reads return marked-deprecated) give push-based staleness detection a hook: a re-push referencing a now-deprecated source id is mechanically detectable at the receiving side.
- **Falsifier:** draft one real elevation as a contract instance. If the contract fields fit badly — elevation needs fields exchange doesn't have, or vice versa — the "same object" conjecture dies and they are two protocols.
- **Novelty:** non-obvious/whitespace — collapses §8 Q4/Q5 and §10 into one primitive. **Attention:** unifying means *one* review discipline to learn, not two.

### H14 — Auto-elevation classes as a MEL-shaped typed register; the retrofit number says build it before entry 1
- **Statement:** The MEL/LCO pattern — pre-declared, typed, time-boxed permitted classes with expiry and one-time extension — could settle "which categories may elevate automatically" by structure: elevation classes declared in advance (e.g. "technique lessons: auto with expiry-until-owner-ratifies," modeled on EASA M.A.801(d)'s provisional commit ratified within seven days; "anything touching health/finance: never auto"), converting per-entry owner review into per-class policy authorized once — and Amendment 1's retrofit cost (19.7%, gold = 13, after 21 months) says the register must exist before the second brain holds its first entry.
- **Mechanism:** the provisional-commit shape fits the owner's stance exactly: auto-elevate into a *provisional* state that expires unratified, so the default failure mode is entry-disappears, not entry-persists-unreviewed. Cluster G is the motive: per-entry gating at real volume recreates ruvnet's 80-nights/4-shipped pathology inside the owner's own memory system.
- **Falsifier:** count actual elevation candidates per month from live runs. If the rate is ~2/month, per-entry review costs nothing, the register is premature, and correct v1 is "owner approves everything." Cheap; should run first.
- **Novelty:** adjacent. **Attention:** the explicit subject — converts O(entries) gates to O(classes) authorizations plus a ratification sweep with a deadline.

### H15 — Source deletion propagates as an un-excisable tombstone; deletion is the second brain's first hard requirement
- **Statement:** Datomic's excision discipline — excision records are themselves protected from excision, "so there is no way to erase your tracks, and every excision creates a permanent record" — could settle §10's correction/deletion question: when a project instance deletes source material, the owner's instance receives a deletion signal through the same contract channel (H13c) and records a tombstone that removes the payload while permanently recording *that* a removal happened, with provenance. This holds the owner's two colliding failure classes apart mechanically instead of trading them off silently.
- **Mechanism:** the tombstone pattern is shipped and characterized; the propagation channel is the same push contract as elevation, so deletion is elevation's inverse on the same rails — no second protocol. Rewind/Limitless demonstrates what no-deletion-authority costs (14-day forced erasure on acquisition; the user held no retention authority at all).
- **Falsifier:** deletion *semantics* may not survive the crossing even if the signal does: if the elevated entry was an attestation (H12) whose text paraphrases the deleted material, tombstoning the reference leaves the paraphrase. Test: delete a source node, propagate, audit the owner instance for residual content. Residue means deletion needs content-aware review — a human gate on every deletion, changing the cost model.
- **Novelty:** non-obvious. **Attention:** deletions become one signal + possible content audit; without this, every source deletion is a manual cross-instance hunt the owner will eventually skip.

### H16 — Inversion: the second brain as an index, not a store
- **Statement:** Inverting §10's premise — the second brain holds *only* the catalog (pointers, grades, sensitivity labels, origin metadata) and never payloads — could improve the §6 failure posture wholesale: an index cannot corrupt payloads it does not hold, cannot expose content it does not contain, and cannot mislead beyond its labels; queries resolve at read time through the coordination substrate with per-query authority.
- **Mechanism:** Amendment 1's A4 number is the motive: 80% of HA's usage concentrates in the top 84 of 1,484 integrations and the median user touches ~2% of the catalogue — a catalogue's value is *coverage probability*, not content possession. If the same shape holds for personal knowledge, copying everything into an aggregate pot buys mostly exposure surface for material that will never be retrieved. `cites:`-as-field is the in-repo precedent for reference-without-merge.
- **Falsifier:** read-time resolution requires source instances online and requires the cross-slug read channel H13 says doesn't exist — and availability is the known killer of exactly this shape (Chumby: servers dark → every device degraded to a clock). Prototype the failure first: what does a second-brain query return when the source repo is archived? "Nothing, permanently" means the index decays into dangling pointers — workflow decay, the one field in Cluster D that failed.
- **Novelty:** non-obvious/whitespace. **Attention:** near-zero elevation gates traded for read-time latency and an availability dependency the owner must operate.

## Cluster V — What Jurati retains; the program as a package

### H17 — Jurati's retained set is the residual of two boundaries that already exist and are already enforced
- **Statement:** The live topology answers §8 Q5 by subtraction rather than design: the workflow ≠ knowledge boundary (ratified) excludes workflows from Unimatrix; per-slug isolation (proven) excludes cross-program knowledge pooling; repos own their definitions. The only unowned residue is exactly **contracts, cross-program workflow state, evidence references, and audit metadata** — the §10 candidate boundary, *derived from enforced constraints rather than proposed as taste*. The leader's addendum makes it concrete: this run needs the `uni` corpus (§7 names it the first workflow corpus) and structurally cannot reach it — the gap Jurati would fill is not hypothetical, it blocked work *today*.
- **Mechanism:** both excluding boundaries are defended for independent reasons (orchestrator-prevention; data isolation), so the residual slot is stable — it does not depend on anyone continuing to agree with Jurati's scope, only on the existing boundaries continuing to hold.
- **Falsifier:** find one object the round trip must carry that is neither knowledge, workflow definition, nor contract/state/evidence-ref/audit — leading candidate is the *disclosed payload in flight*. If payloads must transit *through* Jurati rather than point-to-point, Jurati holds payloads at least transiently, and the clean answer to Q5 is dirty in exactly the place §6's exposure axis cares about most.
- **Novelty:** adjacent, strongly evidenced. **Attention:** bounds what the owner must ever audit inside Jurati.

### H18 — A program's minimal composable surface is four items, and the credential relationship is one of them
- **Statement:** A program-as-package could compose by exposing only: (1) a contract endpoint, (2) a durable program identity key (H2), (3) a published capability catalog with consequence types declared per action *in the program's own action space* (H8), (4) an evidence-grading legend (what its grades mean, for H12 translation) — with agents, skills, workflows and memory staying entirely private, *and with the program holding its own external credential relationships*, because Axis D says whoever must hold the credential for domain N+1 determines who can author it, and Zapier's inversion (9,958 apps vs Thingpedia's 107) is the largest effect size in the scan.
- **Mechanism:** each surface maps to a demonstrated need in the one real exchange — it needed an address, an identity, a capability guess, and a vocabulary translation; the owner performed all four manually (§8). The privacy of everything else is the Zapier/Sandstorm lesson combined: the caller must never need to port into, or hold credentials for, the callee's world.
- **Falsifier:** run the §7 round trip with only these four exposed. If delivery must read the garage's internal protocol files to phrase an acceptable request (vocabulary leakage), the surface is under-specified — and the test is partially blocked today because the `uni` corpus is not on this disk; that blockage is itself the measurement.
- **Novelty:** adjacent. **Attention:** owner stops being the vocabulary translator; cost moves to authoring four surfaces per program, once each.

### H19 — The garage's first composition test is a knowledge request, not a delivery round trip
- **Statement:** A deliberately small first demonstration — arch-research files a contract requesting a *described summary* of the `uni` software-delivery protocols from the Unimatrix program, receiving an evidence-referenced, classification-labeled response — could exercise every composition primitive (identity, bounded deliverable, disclosure decision, classification crossing, acceptance, origin-retaining storage) on a need that already exists, at a fraction of the full delivery-slice cost.
- **Mechanism:** the request is real, not synthetic — §13.5 routes recommendations against a corpus this repo cannot read. Every §8 field gets a concrete value. The response, filed with origin metadata, is simultaneously a live test of H12/H13 elevation semantics: knowledge from one instance, published into another, retaining origin across an enforced isolation boundary.
- **Falsifier:** if the honest answer is "clone the repo to this disk" — the boundary is circumventable by filesystem access at zero cost — the contract ceremony adds nothing and the boundary is theater for co-located repos. **That outcome is itself a finding: which ownership boundaries are real (platform-enforced) vs conventional (agreement-enforced)** — a distinction the whole partition needs and no scout produced.
- **Novelty:** non-obvious — sourced from the leader's addendum; the run's own blockage as the test case. **Attention:** one contract's worth of gates, against learning that currently arrives only by the owner hand-carrying context.

### H20 — Bitemporal × attestation lands naturally on Jurati's smallest component
- **Statement:** The one genuinely open combination in Cluster D — bitemporality joined with attestation, never shipped together, with Rekor v2 having *removed* integrated time — could land on Jurati's contract/state store specifically: contract dispositions are exactly the record type needing both axes (a return *accepted* on date A, evidence *superseded* by the source program on date B, owner's trust record must show what was believed when the §5.7 run was counted), and the store is small, append-only, low-velocity — the easiest conceivable instance of a join that is hard at scale.
- **Mechanism:** Datomic's reified transactions supply the bitemporal half; DSSE the attestation half; the join is unshipped anywhere (S3 and S4 reached this independently). A contract log for one owner is orders of magnitude below the scale at which Rekor v2 found the combination too expensive — the cost argument that killed it elsewhere does not apply.
- **Falsifier:** the cheap-substitute test — keep contract state as files in a git repo and check whether git's commit history (system time) plus in-file dates (valid time) answers every real bitemporal question the trust record asks. If it does, the gap is defensible but not *needed*, and building the join is spent effort — the exact wfh-005 class of error.
- **Novelty:** non-obvious/whitespace. **Attention:** none; it is bookkeeping the owner would otherwise reconstruct by hand at each trust review.

### H21 — Model-authored extensions void the porting-cost objection but relocate it to review
- **Statement:** Cloudflare OS's bet (capability model unchanged from Sandstorm, porting cost removed because *the model writes the extension*) predicts that strong structural authority is nearly free on the *supply* side for this organization — while Cluster G predicts the cost reappears intact on the *review* side (HA banned autonomous contribution because maintainer time is scarcest; FORTIS finds the skill layer is itself a primary escalation source under ordinary, non-adversarial conditions — so model-authored extensions may fail a strong gate persistently, converting suppressed author supply into unbounded review load on the one human).
- **Mechanism:** three incompatible live bets within ten weeks (Umbrel: agents primary contributor; HA: agents banned; Cloudflare: model is author) plus this repo as a fourth stake — disagreement among well-resourced parties is itself the signal that the answer is measurable and unknown. The deciding quantity is gates-cleared-per-owner-hour on model-authored extensions under a capability gate vs without one.
- **Falsifier:** directly measurable in-house: have a model author one bounded extension under a strict declared-capability gate; count review iterations to acceptance. If comparable to ungated authoring, the review-relocation prediction fails and strong gating is nearly free end-to-end.
- **Novelty:** adjacent (the synthesis across A3.2 + Cluster G + FORTIS is in no single scout). **Attention:** this hypothesis *is* the attention-cost question, stated as an experiment.

### H22 — Umbrel's hazard names a constraint for every program package
- **Statement:** Umbrel's structure — the 395-line `SKILL.md` is simultaneously the authoritative packaging contract *and* the instruction the contributing agent reads ("the security boundary and the instruction to the agent are the same document") — implies whatever a program publishes as its composable surface must split into an *enforced* half (schema-validated, checked deterministically at the contract endpoint) and an *advisory* half (guidance the model reads), because a boundary existing only as text the bounded party interprets is Cluster H's inert-control pattern by construction — the same shape as MCP's `destructiveHint` (advisory, untrusted by its own spec) against Apple's `IntentAuthenticationPolicy` (author-declared, OS-enforced).
- **Mechanism:** Cluster B's sharpest contrast plus six Cluster H instances, including one in the SDK this garage runs on (#172, open 5½ months). **This repo currently runs the Umbrel pattern** — `.claude/` files are both boundary and instruction — so the hypothesis indicts the dogfood surface too.
- **Falsifier:** audit one of this repo's own protocol gates: is any gate outcome enforced by something other than the model having read the gate's description? If yes (a hook, a permission rule), the split already partially exists and the hypothesis under-credits the current design; document where the line actually falls.
- **Novelty:** adjacent. **Attention:** the enforced half is what lets the owner *not* re-read every agent-facing document to know what's guaranteed.

## The strongest case for consolidation — presented at full strength, per assignment

1. **Schema evolution is the graveyard, and federation is what filled it.** Anytype fixes its interop wall on a two-month cadence *because it owns client, server, protocol and schema*; Solid — maximally federated, sovereignty-first — diagnosed the same wall in 2019 and its fix is **still a v0.2 draft seven years later**. Chandler's schema could never be settled; Urbit dodged schema and paid in per-agent coupling. Every §8 field, every H11 carrier, every H12 grade legend is a schema that will need to evolve. A one-owner federation of three repos is *a committee of one person wearing three hats* — federation's coordination cost with none of federation's reason to exist. Consolidation buys Anytype's cadence at zero sovereignty cost, because sovereignty against yourself is incoherent.
2. **Every boundary crossing is owner attention, and the owner is the measured bottleneck.** Composition without consolidation *maximizes* crossings — each with identity, classification, acceptance and escalation events. 4. **The 2-year window is the cheap time to consolidate.** Amendment 1's retrofit number cuts both ways: if the federated structure is wrong, unwinding later means re-homing catalogues at a measured 19.7%-in-21-months recruitment rate. One user, three small repos, ~229 nodes: consolidation today is an afternoon; after five programs and a populated second brain it is a rewrite.

**The counter-counter-point, for symmetry:** the same evidence shows consolidated systems died of what consolidation costs — vendor-held `/etc` as a kill switch (Chumby, Mycroft/Selene, Rabbit) — and §6's failure classes are per-domain sensitivity partitions one flat instance structurally cannot express.
