## 0. Stance — written to be read by the owner

**Would I send this packet to the human as-is? Yes. It is the most instrument-honest run this theme
has produced, and I would not hold it for rework.**

The run answered the question the theme actually needed answered. V5 was hand-converged across two
model tracks, dated the day the run started, and had never consumed a single instance. A third
argument-level review would have produced a third argument. Instantiation-plus-rejection is the
smallest instrument that could have made the model say something falsifiable, and it did: 18 fixtures
violate a rule V5 states and its own checker admits them; one required value of a core entity has no
conforming home; zero of fifty OPEN items were resolved; `Capability` has no clean witness anywhere in
the run. None of that was reachable by argument. **ALIGNED on drift.**

**But the verdict word is the least informative thing in the packet, and the owner should not rule on
it.** `revise` was the near-certain outcome before the run began — the SCOPE's `retain` bar requires
*every* material semantic to fit typed structure, which one closed vocabulary defeats, and its
`reject` bar requires an eighth core entity, which nothing short of a domain change would produce.
`revise` is the middle box, and landing in it records that the model is not finished. It does not move
the theme. What moves the theme is two findings the run produced almost as by-products, and the
synthesis must lead with those rather than with the verdict:

1. **V5's discriminating power is exactly co-extensive with its typed references.** Nine of the
   eighteen accepted defects are cases where *both operands are already typed* and the model simply
   never writes the sentence that joins them. The exclusions that hold are the ones carried by type or
   closure; the ones carried by prose against a registry are re-openable by exactly the program the
   exclusion binds. W3 states the general form: *a typed reference buys a check and a text field buys
   nothing.* That is a design law for any common operating model, not a fact about V5, and it is the
   most transferable thing the run produced.
2. **Neither case contains one instance of enforced authority.** `Delegation.enforced_by` is empty on
   31 of 31 in the research case; `EffectBoundary.enforces` is empty in both. The only refusal receipt
   in the entire research history is a *schema* refusal, not an authority refusal. The theme exists to
   keep consequential authority outside the agents being governed — and two owner-operated histories,
   read exhaustively against a model built to express exactly that, contain no witness that it has
   ever been enforced anywhere. This is a first-order finding about **the organization**, not the
   model, and the run correctly declined to let it move the model verdict. That is not a reason to let
   it disappear.

**Where I part company with the run is not on evidence. It is on case selection.** Both instances are
owner-operated histories, inside one organization, authored under this garage's own method, largely by
the same agent lineage. Every result in the packet is therefore compatible with V5 being an elaborate,
well-tested description of how *we* work. The run says so itself, four times, and `open.semantic_
compression` stays open "by construction." **UNDER-REACH — one named level-up, and it is worth it:
the next instance should be a history this organization did not author, chosen because it contains
real enforced refusals** (§2).

On the run's own reflexive defect — its instruments repeatedly trusting a label instead of the value
under it — my judgment is that this is **evidence the theme is aimed at something real, and it is the
strongest such evidence the theme holds.** It also warrants a **specific, bounded discount**, which
lands on the run's *coverage* claims and not on its *adverse* findings, for a structural reason (§3).
The two conclusions are not in tension, and the second is not a hedge on the first.

**Advisory verdict: ALIGNED on drift, with a framing correction. UNDER-REACH on case selection.
Recommend the owner accept the packet, refuse the V6 authoring move it will invite, and spend the
next run on a foreign case.** Full stance at §8.

## 1. Drift assessment — **ALIGNED**, with a framing correction the owner must apply

### 1.1 The target was right

The theme needed to know whether the compression V5 proposes carries behaviour or merely relocates
domain meaning into prose. That question is only answerable by consumption. The run did not substitute
an easier question for it:

- It did not ask "is V5 a good model." The anti-force-fitting amendment was built precisely to close
  that escape, and W4 found **zero circular conformance** — both workstreams identified and rejected
  their *own* circular candidate before the adjudicator reached them, and the two changes V5 would
  most prefer (`asserted` → `claimed`; inventing a `done_when` for `jurati-arch-002`) are the two that
  were refused.
- It did not convert missing enforcement into model failure. Every enforcement absence is reported as
  absence, and W4 shows the discrimination is load-bearing: **the verdict moved only on `model-defect`
  rows.** Enforcement gaps (33) and evidence gaps (85) are recorded and explicitly did not move it.
- It did not normalise a single OPEN item into a convenient default. Both `resolved-by-instance` claims
  raised in the run were contradicted by the other workstream and overruled. **Zero** OPEN items are
  resolved by these instances.

Each of those is a place where a run of this shape usually drifts. This one holds at all three.

One structural point in the run's favour that nobody in the packet claims: **the model under test was
authored outside the funnel.** V5's own `meta.track` reads `converged`, its derivation names four
lineage files and a target-concept document, and those files were untracked working-tree artifacts
until this run committed them byte-for-byte at `6718049`. wfh-011 is the first mechanical contact
between the funnel and a hand-converged owner-track artifact. Subjecting an owner-authored model to
the garage's own adversarial machinery — rather than ratifying it — is the funnel working as designed,
and it is worth naming because the theme's recorded history (wfh-005, wfh-002) is that owner-injected
material has repeatedly bypassed the method.

### 1.2 Does `revise` move the theme, or record that the model is not finished?

**It records that the model is not finished, and on its own it moves nothing.** Three reasons:

- **The bar was constructed so that `revise` was overwhelmingly the prior.** `retain` needs *every*
  material semantic to fit typed structure or a conforming registered extension — a single closed
  vocabulary lacking an extension point defeats it, and `values.evidence_grade` (closed on
  `[missing, claimed, partial, proven]` against a program vocabulary containing `asserted`, with no
  registry and no `extension_owner`) does exactly that. `reject` needs an eighth core entity or a
  collapsed one, which two same-organization histories in adjacent domains were never going to
  produce. The outcome space was effectively `{revise}` before W1 wrote a line.
- **`current-project fit` and `post-bounded-evolution fit` are identical**, and W4 is right that the
  identity is the sharper result: **not one of the ten surviving evolution candidates repairs a single
  model defect.** They improve evidence, reproducibility and recovery; they repair no construct. So
  the counterfactual arm — the most expensive machinery the amendment added — returned "no better
  answer exists over there." That is a genuine and useful negative, and it is also the arm least
  likely to be read correctly from a one-word verdict.
- **The verdict's natural next move is a trap.** "Revise" invites authoring V6 against the named
  defect list. V6 would be produced by the same argument process that produced V5, and would then need
  a wfh-012 to instantiate it. That is the review-and-revise treadmill wfh-011 was created to break,
  re-entered one step further along. The defect list is the run's most useful concrete output *as a
  design constraint*, not as a work order.

**The framing correction I am asking the owner to apply:** rule on §0's two findings, not on the word
`revise`. If the packet is summarised as "the model needs another revision round," the run's yield is
lost and the theme buys a V6.

### 1.3 One drift-adjacent observation, not a drift verdict

The coordinator objective names *the smallest operating model **and technical substrate***. wfh-011 is
entirely operating-model, which is correct for a run scoped this way — and I checked before making the
usual complaint: the external surface is **not** stale. wfh-007, a recurring four-surface scan, closed
2026-08-28, one day before this run. The watchlist's `last looked` column reading 2026-08-01 is a

## 2. Under-reach assessment — **UNDER-REACH**, one level-up, recommendation **pursue**

### 2.1 Careful-and-bounded was the right call for the depth axis

I want to be clear about what I am *not* saying. Two instantiations, an adversarial suite, an
independent cross-case adjudicator and two independent coverage audits, at zero external cost, is
proportionate. Going wide before the model had consumed one instance would have been the wrong
instrument. And the run's honesty machinery — every checker re-executed rather than read from its log,
every artifact reproducing byte-identically, a guard that *reduced* its own claimed coverage by 30
rows and increased it on none, 69 conformance errors published rather than repaired away — is a
property a larger and looser run would not have had. **The depth was right.**

### 2.2 The level-up that was available and was not taken: case selection

**Both cases were chosen because we had them, not because of what they could falsify.** That is the
under-reach, and it is a selection decision, not an effort decision.

The consequences are measurable in the run's own artifacts:

- **205 of 679 canonical X rows rest on a single case** — W1's enumeration was coarser and never
  reached them; W4 reconciled them from W2 alone.
- **Nothing in the run witnesses a versioned `Capability`**, and `Capability` has no fully conforming
  witness in either case: W1 instantiates zero; W2's two carry `grade: UNREPRESENTABLE` and
  `done_when: missing-history`, both required fields.
- **Three `Capability` OPEN items are `not-exercised`** — recursive evidence rollup, parent-child
  `done_when`, `open.capability_rollup`. `wfh-010` left `capability_rollup` unexecuted, and this run
  leaves it unexecuted too. Two consecutive runs have failed to reach the same construct.
- **The entire custody half of the model is unwitnessed.** `EffectBoundary.enforces` empty in both;
  `can_refuse` read by no rule in M01; `Delegation.enforced_by` empty on 31 of 31; I12's second clause
  with no witness anywhere. These are the constructs that carry the theme's *stated load-bearing
  boundary*, and the run has tested them only negatively.
- **`open.semantic_compression` stays open "by construction."** Two owner-operated histories inside one
  organisation cannot settle a generality claim in either direction, and both workstreams say so.

**The level-up, named:** instantiate the pinned V5 against a **third history this organisation did not
author**, selected specifically because it contains **real enforced refusals** — a public
forge-enforced delivery record with required checks that actually blocked, a regulated or
safety-critical process with refusal receipts, or a standards-body decision record with independent
gate custody. Read-only, pinned alphabet, zero external cost.

**What it buys — a step-function, not a linear gain.** It changes the *class* of claim available.

- Today the run can say *representable here.* One foreign case is the only thing that can convert that
  into a portability claim — and it is the only instrument that can move `open.semantic_compression`
  off "open by construction," which no number of additional owner-operated cases can do.
- A case with real refusals supplies the **first witness for the half of V5 that carries the theme's
  own boundary.** Right now every custody, effect and enforced-authority result in the theme is a
  negative from a corpus that has never contained a positive. A model whose custody constructs have
  only ever been tested against histories with no custody has not been tested.
- It attacks the run's biggest hole and its biggest unexamined assumption **with one instrument**.

**Cost — the "is it worth it" the check requires.** One directional workstream. The instruments already
exist and are proven: `wfh-008-validate.py` accepts any number of instance files against the pinned
model, `v5_instance_check.py` runs the 115-fixture suite from M01 alone, `w4-witness.py` reads both
encodings natively, and `w4-build-ledger.py` regenerates the 899 KB ledger byte-for-byte. The only new
cost is pinning a foreign corpus into a fixed alphabet, which the leader has already done once for
`dug-21/unimatrix`. Zero external cost. This is cheap in absolute terms and very cheap relative to
authoring and re-testing a V6.

**Recommendation: PURSUE — as a separate directional scope, ahead of any V6 authoring, with the case
selected for what it would falsify.** This is a frontier-change proposal, advisory. It does not
re-open wfh-011 and it does not relax any bar.

### 2.3 A second, smaller opportunity — recommendation **defer-and-record**

W3's suite was never run against the two counterfactual evolved forms. The amendment permitted it and
did not require it; W4 records the gap and explains why it was not W3's fault (W1's instance was absent
for W3's whole run). The sharpest instance: W2/CF-05's own recorded residual — *a required check whose
predicate is "a file exists" is satisfiable by writing a file* — is **exactly** the shape W3's suite is
built to falsify, and it was not falsified. Cheap, named by the run itself, and currently a hole.
**Record it as an architect-for-future item so it is not lost; do not spend a run on it alone.**

### 2.4 What I am explicitly *not* recommending

- **Not** a V6 authoring pass. §1.2.
- **Not** building the enforcement point the run keeps circling. The scope forbids it, and I agree with
  the refusal on the merits: the missing credential boundary cannot be costed from any alphabet this
  theme has assembled, and proposing it now would be a build recommendation dressed as a finding.
- **Not** a third owner-operated case. It would produce the same class of result at the same cost.

## 3. The run-specific question — its instruments failed the way the theme says instruments fail

**My judgment: this is evidence the theme is aimed at something real — the strongest such evidence the
theme holds — and it warrants a specific, bounded discount that reaches the run's coverage claims and
not its adverse findings. Both, not either.**

### 3.1 It is not a coincidence, and the reason it is not is the interesting part

The instances, from the packet:

1. Two validators printed green on `vnc-045-instance.yaml` while parsing **zero** objects from it.
2. `vnc-045-coverage-build.py` never opened its own instance — 596 `exercised` dispositions, 346
   carrying the provenance string *"see instance"*, joined to nothing.
3. W4's reconciler applied its rule to the **disposition** column and never the **value** column.
4. W1's generator counts key presence, not value population — `north_star: []` read as *"set on 2/2."*
   This was on the side round 1 had credited as sound.
5. W1 asserted a fact about `origin` from a local mirror of it (`git branch -a --contains` is not
   evidence about a remote; `git ls-remote` is).
6. The leader did the same thing twice, in two successive messages, and withdrew both.
7. W1 updated a ledger and did not re-read the paragraph three lines above that *counts* the ledger
   rows; the reviewer who caught it was reading a mutable working tree and reported it as committed
   state. Two agents, one file, one error.
8. Round 2's own new findings: `core.Capability.versioned` `exercised` with no witness in either case,
   and — the sharpest one — the arithmetic in §13.3, the single paragraph whose entire purpose is
   disclosing what the guard did **not** cover, does not reproduce.

Eight occurrences, one shape: **a check accepting a label in place of the value it names, where the
label was produced by the party with an interest in it.** Every agent involved had read `#320` and was
explicitly on guard against this predicate. It happened anyway, in the leader, in three of four
workstreams, in the adjudicator, and inside a disclosure of scope.

That is not coincidence. Eight independent occurrences among primed agents on a task whose entire
subject is that failure is a **base rate**, and it is measured rather than reconstructed —
first-party, observed, with the commands recorded. The theme's central claim is that *a control whose
input sits inside the governed party is a label, not a control.* Every prior treatment of that claim
in this theme has been a design argument. **wfh-011 is the first time it has a rate and a set of
instances.**

And there is a second-order fact that carries more weight than the count: **the repair was the same
every time — join the check to the value instead of the label — and every repair reduced claimed
coverage rather than increasing it.** That is one-to-one with the model finding in §0.1 (*a typed
reference buys a check; prose buys nothing*). The instrument failure and the model failure are the
same failure at two altitudes. If the hypothesis is true, that is exactly what you would expect to
see, and seeing it is corroboration, not embarrassment.

**Where this must go.** W4 §9.1 ruled the observation out of the ledger and it was right to: it is not
evidence about `wfh-008` or `vnc-045`, and admitting it would risk it being read as observed case
coverage. But the ledger is not the only destination, and routing it to "the retro" files the theme's
single strongest empirical result as a note about carelessness. **It is not a lesson about
carelessness. It is a measurement of the theme's hypothesis, and it should reach the owner as
evidence.** That is my one substantive disagreement with the packet's disposition of its own material.

### 3.2 The discount — specific, and it stops where the evidence changes character

Discounting has to name what it reaches, or it is mood. Where the defect actually bit, the evidence
*is* weaker, and the round-2 auditor has already priced most of it:

- `vnc-045-instance.yaml` carries **69 conformance errors** against M01 as parsed — 55 of 210 declared
  inverses not closing, 11 Delegations missing required `effective_at`, 3 `WF-01.binds` targets that
  do not exist. Every claim resting on the software case rests on an instance with that error load.
- The software case's declared-inverse coverage is **22 of 34**, not what its matrix implies; the run's
  is **28 of 34**. Any "the software case exercises N inverses" taken from that matrix is wrong by up
  to 12.
- **W1's 468-row matrix was never regenerated** after §13.4 found the same defect in its generator. The
  reconciled ledger is protected at witness-bearing paths because the guard measures the instance
  directly; W1's own matrix is not.
- W2's `T01`/`T02` are **living, unversioned protocol files** read at `37c7b09a`, not at the delivery
  date. Every `Workflow`, `Gate`, `Role` and `Delegation` verdict in the software case inherits that,
  and nothing in the alphabet can date it.
- Every `vnc-045` Delegation is an inference; `resource_ceiling` has no source at all.

**And the limit the packet does not state: the discovery curve did not flatten.** Round 1 found 6
zero-witness rows. Round 2's guard found **33 more beyond the six**, plus two new classes (N4, N5) the
audit had not reached. Each independent look found more than the last. The rework rounds were capped
at two **by the SCOPE**, not by exhaustion, and the `PASS` says the four named items discharged — not
that the class is closed. There is no mechanism in the run that bounds the residual. So: **treat every
coverage number in this packet as a floor on defects and a ceiling on coverage, never the reverse.**

### 3.3 Why the discount does not reach the verdict — and this is the load-bearing part

The failure was in the **coverage instruments** — the things that count *what was tested* — and not in
the **rejection instrument**, which is what produced the adverse findings. Those are different
artifacts with different provenance:

- The 115-fixture suite is derived **from M01 alone**, deliberately, and W3 kept W1's and W2's
  instances out of its baseline precisely to protect the divergence analysis. It re-executes
  byte-identically. The 18 accepted defects are executed checker output, independent of both case
  matrices.
- The single strongest model-defect — `evidence_grade` closed against a term the program has ruled on
  — was verified **at source** by W4, reading `SCOPE-RISK-ASSESSMENT.md` SR-10 at `37c7b09a` and
  Issue #928 comment `4898695344` directly. It does not pass through a coverage table at any point.
- "No row requires an eighth core entity" is a negative over 679 reconciled rows *and* 115 executed
  fixtures. A coverage instrument that over-credits `exercised` cannot manufacture that negative; if
  anything it would conceal a positive.

So the discount lands on **how much of the model was exercised, and by which case** — and not on **what
broke**. That is the right shape, and it is why I do not discount the verdict or the defect list.

### 3.4 The behavioural evidence, which I weigh above the ruling

The cheapest way through the round-2 gate was to edit `vnc-045-instance.yaml`: add three Gate objects,
eleven `effective_at` values and fifty-five back-references, and publish a clean run. **Both instance
digests are byte-identical before and after, and the run published the 69 errors instead.** All 30
reconciled-disposition changes moved *away* from `exercised`; **zero rows gained it.** W4 found and
published the same defect on W1's side — its own case's side, which the audit had credited as sound —
unprompted. W2 refused to widen its fix beyond the four classes RW-1 named, and said so.

A run that cleared a gate by quietly repairing the artifact its corrected instrument complained about
would look nothing like this. **That is worth more than the `PASS`**, and it is the reason I am
comfortable relaying a packet whose instruments demonstrably failed.

## 5. What this run should be used for — and what it must not

### Legitimate uses

1. **As a design constraint on any successor model.** The 18 accepted defects, and especially the nine
   where both operands are already typed, are a specific and cheap repair list. More importantly the
   *general* form — put the semantics in a typed reference or accept that they are unenforceable — is

2. **As the theme's first-order finding about the organisation:** two owner-operated histories contain
   zero enforced authority. That is about us, not about V5, and it is actionable independently of any
   model decision.
3. **As a proven instrument set.** Checkers, witness extractor and ledger generator all regenerate
   byte-identically from committed sources. Any subsequent case is far cheaper than this one was.
4. **As measured method evidence** for the label-versus-value defect, at a rate, on primed agents (§3).
5. **As a negative on the counterfactual arm:** ten surviving project-evolution candidates, none of
   which repairs a model defect. Changing the projects would not have saved the model.

### Uses that are not supported by this evidence

1. **No generality, portability or semantic-compression claim.** Two owner-operated histories in one
   organisation. `open.semantic_compression` is open by construction and this run did not touch it.
2. **No enforcement claim of any kind.** No historical project ever refused anything on V5's account.
   Every "rejection" in the packet is a property of a checker written this week from a YAML file.
3. **No grade movement, no `proven`, no node, no edge.** The scope forbids it; the run performed none;
   I recommend none.
4. **No coverage number taken from a case matrix.** Use the reconciled ledger. Specifically: the
   software case's inverse coverage is 22 of 34 (the run's is 28 of 34), and W1's 468-row matrix is
   still produced by an uncorrected generator.
5. **No claim about `Capability`.** No fully conforming witness anywhere; nothing witnesses a versioned
   `Capability`; three `Capability` OPEN items are `not-exercised` for the second run running.
6. **Not authority to author V6**, and not a build recommendation at any depth.
7. **No claim that the coverage defect class is closed.** The `PASS` says four named items discharged.
   The discovery curve did not flatten (§3.2).

## 7. Gap check — does the packet imply the decomposition missed something?

Two things, both advisory.

**(a) The case-selection axis is not in the decomposition at all.** W1/W2/W3/W4 partition *depth* —
two instantiations, a rejection suite, an adjudicator. Nothing in the scope's workstream structure ever
asks *which cases would falsify this*, so the answer defaulted to the two we had. That is the gap §2.2
names, and it is a decomposition gap rather than an execution failure. A future instantiation scope
should carry a case-selection workstream whose output is a justified choice, not an availability list.

**(b) The theme's V5 track has produced zero graph nodes across two runs.** I confirmed by read-only
search: nothing from wfh-010 or wfh-011 is in Unimatrix. This is *correct* under a scope that forbids
writes, and I am not faulting the run. But `CLAUDE.md` names the KB as the compounding asset, and this
track's entire yield is file-resident. At least three findings here are graph-shaped, durable and
domain-independent — the typed-reference law, zero enforced authority in two histories, and the
label-versus-value rate. **The owner should decide whether a bounded, write-enabled distillation is
warranted;** the decision is not mine, and nothing in this review authorises a write.
